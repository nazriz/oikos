- **Births are the one thing held back:** they need a contract decision (question 1).

- **Perception test:** it compared periodic channels that hadn't been looked through again that tick.

## Questions for you

1. **Sex as physics (blocks births).**
   - The occupancy standard needs children's sex, and a parent must know their newborn's. As a trait (now), a parent can't know it.
   - **Proposal:** bodies get an optional `sex`. The kernel draws a newborn's from the world's sex ratio (`life.birth`), birth checks who can bear a child, and a parent sees their children in their body's view.
   - Then: fertility from ABS age-specific birth rates, a child template, and parents bringing newborns home.
   - Alternative: leave births out for now.
   - This is a biological attribute for physics only; gender and identity stay content.
2. **Sign off the rest of the v0.4 draft?** The choices made while building are listed in D47–D48:
   - capacity per act, not per call;
   - lots filled in by corner;
   - new call functions;
   - `block.cells` and `block.lot` for formal law;
   - `pick` traits;
   - `as` on propose and message.
   Then freeze v0.4.
3. **When a leaseholder dies,** the household can't tell. The rent keeps being paid from the dead person's account, and nobody takes over the lease. Options:
   - **(a)** perception: people see *why* someone left a space they share (died, left, evicted);
   - **(b)** content: a registry of deaths, with someone who reports them;
   - **(c)** leave it (about one household a run).
   I'd lean (a): it's physical, since you'd know if your partner died at home.
4. **Citizens' voice still cascades.** A tenant petition starts on day 0–1 and, now that it's measured against adults, reaches ~99% of them. The Granovetter thresholds (mean 25%) are illustrative, not evidence. Calibrate against real petition-signing and turnout? It's the checkpoint's item 3; I'd do it with the word-of-mouth job gap.
5. **Calibration targets to adopt:**
   - private renters spend 20.2% of gross income on housing, and 58% of lower-income private renters spend over 30% (ABS *Housing Occupancy and Costs* 2019–20);
   - measured: about 45% of the town's households have two or more spare bedrooms, because households take the dearest home they can afford.
   Should rent stress and spare bedrooms become calibration targets, like the word-of-mouth ones?
6. **AI welfare (§15) with bodies:**
   - A language-model person can now fall ill, be hurt and die. They're debriefed if configured, but should a model be told its body's conditions at all under honest framing?
   - `harm` exists as an act. Should it be withheld from language-model participants by default, or offered and observed? (No code citizen uses it; the town doesn't model it yet.)
7. **The real-model pilot** (checkpoint question 1): still recommended before Stage 3.
   - Before it: the Anthropic adapter with a spending guard, and a translator test harness. I've held these, as agreed (mock until you raise it).
   - One seed, one year, projected at about $0.71 on Sonnet 5 at mock token counts (real replies are longer).
8. **What next:**
   - (a) births, once question 1 is settled;
   - (b) calibration: voice, word of mouth, housing;
   - (c) the pilot;
   - (d) Stage 3, the renderer.
   My order: 1 and 2 (your answers), then the pilot, then Stage 3, with calibration alongside.

## Smaller gaps (recorded, not blocking)

- **Package name:** `seats/` (the package `@terrarium/seats`) keeps its name, though "seat" is gone everywhere else. Renaming is mechanical; say if you want it.
- **Illness rates** are a labelled placeholder. AIHW's age-specific overnight rates are in its data cubes, not its web pages.
- **Renter-specific household shares** are likewise in ABS data cubes; the town uses the whole population's.
- **Households don't yet form or end:** partnering, separating, adult children leaving home, flatmates leaving.
- **Nobody acts on illness yet:** sick leave, the National Employment Standards' 10 days.
- **Migration:** almost nobody comes to the 1,000-person town, because locals fill vacancies within the month. That's the Harris–Todaro logic working, but the outside income and response rate are placeholders.
- **Kernel time** is now mostly applying acts (40%), monthly state checkpoints (22%), and building and hashing observations (21%). Nothing cheap is left; checkpoints could become incremental if runs get bigger.
