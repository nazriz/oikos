# Culture: what a town holds in common

Design: [docs/design/ai-citizens.md](../design/ai-citizens.md) section 14. Code: `participants/src/behaviour/culture.ts` (the arithmetic), `participants/src/behaviour/citizen.ts` (where a person holds, learns and acts on it), `worlds/generator/generate.ts` (the dial, and origin). Decisions: docs/decisions.md, 2026-10-13, "Culture: plan" and "Culture: building it". Sources: [docs/research/references.md](../research/references.md), "Culture". **Off by default**: `living.culture` in a spec, `dials.culture` in the world config. With it off no line of this is entered and the world is byte-identical to the world without it.

**Nothing here is the town's.** Every figure is one person's belief, kept in their own record. What a town holds in common is read from logs, outside (design 14h). The platform judges nothing: no conduct is the one a good town has, and no group is better or worse at anything. Every size is a **placeholder** unless this page names a source; they are one set for every conduct, and parameters of the dial (`dials.culture.step`).

## What a person holds

For each **conduct** their world gives them (a closed list in code, at most 12; each tied to one draw the code mind already makes):

| Conduct | The occasion; "does" is | Draw it sways |
|---|---|---|
| `answer_tribunal` | served with an application for possession; answers it | `possession.answer_bps` (`answer-<lease>`) |
| `stay_past_notice` | told to vacate, cannot pay; still there at the date | `possession.stay_bps` (`stay-<lease>`) |
| `take_to_tribunal` | an unlawful rent rise, or a debt unpaid; applies | `tribunal.challenge_bps`, `apply_bps` (through `reads`) |
| `complain` | a service failed; writes to the town | `complain_bps` (through `reads`) |
| `take_in` | asked for a roof by someone known; says yes | `stay.willing_affinity_bps` |
| `join_union` | a new job; joins | `union_join_bps` |
| `strike` | a ballot for action; votes yes | `strike_vote_yes_bps` |
| `take_from_employer` | short of money at work among goods; takes some | `crime.take_bps` |
| `apply_possession`, `heads_before_hours`, `order_possession` | an organisation's conducts: a person may hold a view of them; no decision of a person uses them | none |

Three conducts asked for are not choices anyone makes today (paying rent before other things, paying rates, reporting a neighbour) and wait for the choice.

The numbers (basis points; whole percent in the record):
- `does`: of those I know of who met the occasion, the share who did it (Bicchieri's empirical expectation; Cialdini's descriptive norm);
- `approves`: how far I take those around me to approve of doing it (the normative expectation; the injunctive norm);
- `own`: what I think of it myself, from my values; `says`: what I say when asked;
- `n`: how much I have seen, as a weight held to `window` (20).

**How it starts.** `does` and `approves` start at the profile's (`values.culture.conducts.<conduct>.does_bps / approves_bps`); where it gives no `does_bps` it is the conduct's own rate in this world, so the picture people start with is the town as calibrated; where it gives no `approves_bps`, 5,000, a named placeholder. A person from an origin group whose profile gives group figures starts from those instead. Weight `prior_weight` (4). `own` starts at the person's starting `approves`, plus their own lean (a normal draw of sd `own_sd_bps` 1,500 on their own stream, `culture-own-<conduct>`) and `values_bps` (500) per standard deviation of the values that speak to the conduct (`VALUE_MAP` in `culture.ts`: ours, to test against data, as needs-from-values is). **`n` and a conduct a person holds:** a conduct is held where the world gives the person a rate for it or the profile a figure; `take_in` has no rate, so it starts at the placeholder 5,000.

**Starting attitudes from the profiles (2026-10-13; step 8 of the plan, moved up).** Until now no profile gave `approves_bps`, so everyone began at exactly 5,000 with a symmetric lean and a town's mean could not move. All nine profiles now carry `values.culture.conducts` for four conducts, `approves_bps` taken from the confidence the profile's own `outlook.trust` records (World Values Survey and European Values Study, joint wave 7, as Our World in Data publishes it; to confirm against the survey's tables), and the mapping is the profile's data, with its weakness, in `sources["culture.conducts"]`:

| Conduct | Item of `outlook.trust` | Why that is a proxy for approval |
|---|---|---|
| `answer_tribunal`, `take_to_tribunal` | `courts` | where the courts are trusted, answering a case there and taking a wrong to it are what people think is right (Tyler 1990: people comply with and use institutions they hold legitimate) |
| `complain` | `civil_service` | writing to the town is approved where its officers are trusted to answer |
| `join_union` | `unions` | joining a body one has confidence in |

The figure is where **both** the picture of what others approve and the person's own lean begin (own = the figure plus the personal spread and the values, as before); the lean the decision uses is measured from the figure, so every rate starts as calibrated. **How sure:** low. Confidence in an institution is not approval of an act toward it; the share above a threshold is read as a mean, as `outlook` already does; the figures are national, not the place's, and the survey's own tables were not opened (the Our World in Data summary was). **Not given** (a conduct then starts from values alone, at 5,000, as before): `strike`, `take_in`, `stay_past_notice`, `take_from_employer`, and the organisations' conducts; the survey's items on joining strikes and on whether stealing property is justifiable exist and were not opened. **By origin group:** none; no source was found that gives group figures, so `by_group` is empty everywhere. Nothing in code names a place or a figure; `orchestrator/test/culture-start.test.ts` holds the mapping to the outlook and the start to the figure. **A limit that stays:** with one figure for both the picture and the lean, the mean of what is said is still its start; what can move a mean is an asymmetry (a group whose figure differs, a hearing from outside, a published count), and none of those is given here without a source.

**What I think and what I say.** `own` settles half the way to `approves` every `internalise_days` (1,095), worked out when read (nothing runs daily; Kelman 1958 on compliance becoming internalised). `says` is `own` moved toward `approves` by `pressure` of the way (rewritten 2026-10-13, "Culture: the gap between what is thought and what is said"; it was `belonging x (1 - integrity) x 4`, a scale carried over from `Inner.view`).

**What I think and what I say, as it now is.** Kuran (1995, *Private Truths, Public Lies*): a person chooses what to say publicly to balance two things, the reputation lost by saying what the audience does not approve (which counts for more the more one cares what others think) and the inner cost of misstating what one thinks (which counts for more the more one holds out for one's own mind). Each cost is taken to grow with the square of its distance (a modelling choice of ours; Kuran gives the two costs and leaves the form open, **to confirm**), so the best thing to say is a weighted mean of what I think and what I take the street to approve:

`pressure = R / (R + I)`, `says = own + pressure x (approves - own)`, with `R = reputation_scale_milli / 1000 x belonging x conformity` and `I = integrity` (each 0 to 1).

- **Meaning of the scale.** `pressure` is the share of the way from my view to the street's that I go aloud. It is 0 for someone who does not care what the street thinks or who has every bit of integrity, 1 for someone with none, a half where the two weigh alike. `reputation_scale_milli` (default 1,000) is how much the cost of standing out weighs against the cost of misstating, in thousandths: at 1,000 they weigh alike when belonging and conformity are both at 1 and integrity is 1; at 2,000 reputation weighs twice as much. It is the one dial of the rule and is a parameter of the dial (`dials.culture.step.reputation_scale_milli`).
- **Real-world basis.** Who conforms aloud and how much: in Asch's line-judgment trials about a third of the critical answers followed an unanimous wrong majority, three quarters of people did at least once, and a quarter never (Asch 1956, *Psychological Monographs* 70(9); Bond and Smith 1996, *Psychological Bulletin* 119(1), a meta-analysis of 133 studies in 17 countries, to confirm figures). With belonging, conformity and integrity at their typical middle (a half each) the rule gives a `pressure` of a third, which is that order of magnitude; the default was not chosen to get that or any outcome, it is the equal footing of the two costs, and the typical value follows from the persons' own figures.
- **A view farther from the street is moved by more points** (the same share of a longer way); a person with the street's view says it whatever their pressure.
- **What was wrong before.** The old gap, `belonging x (1 - integrity) x 4`, took its `4` from `Inner.view`, where the pressure is a product of three fractions (how much they could cost me, how afraid I am, how little I trust them) and rarely near 1. Here the factor was a single weight of belonging, so it saturated at 1 for 57% of people, who then said exactly what they took the street to approve: 97 to 99% of everything said was "neither right nor wrong" and verdicts were nearly never voiced. That was a scale in the wrong place, not a calibration. The new rule has no free constant of that kind.
- **Pluralistic ignorance still follows**, for a person with little integrity who cares and follows (pressure near 1); for the rest, part of what they think leaks into what they say, and a street hears it. That is the mechanism working: the leak is how a norm shifts.

**The record.** One section, `norms`, **positional**: an array of at most 12 places, the place in the closed list of conducts (`CONDUCTS` in `culture.ts`) being the conduct, each a row `[does, approves, own, says, n]` in whole percent, `null` where the person holds none, trailing nulls left off (`normsRows` reads it back as named rows). Written again only when a figure has moved by 2 in 100 (`grain_percent`). It does not grow with the town or with time. **Why positional (2026-10-13, step 0):** the kernel canonicalises and hashes a person's whole record at every change to it, so any section costs its bytes each time anything in the record is written, moved or not. Named rows were about 400 bytes of a 1,700-byte record (a quarter of it); positional rows are about 120, and a record carrying the section is 16% bigger than one without, where it was 32%. The log carries only a changed section, so it is unaffected.

## The term at a decision

```
lean = ( c * (w_does * (does - does0) + w_approves * (approves - approves0)) + (1 - c) * (own - approves0) ) / sd_bps
rate' = rate * (1 + sway * lean)        (lean held to three standard deviations; the rate to 0..10000)
```
`c` is the person's conformity (0 to 1); `does0`, `approves0` the profile's (the point the picture is measured from); `sd_bps` 2,500; `w_does` 0.6, `w_approves` 0.4 (where what is done and what is approved part, people follow what is done: Bicchieri and Xiao 2009; the sizes are placeholders); `sway` 0.3 (as trust and agency have). A conformist follows the street; someone who is not follows themself. **No draw is added, moved or renamed:** the same draw meets a shifted rate. **With every figure at the profile's the lean is the person's own alone, which sums to nothing over a town** (tested over 3,000 drawn people), so the calibrated rates hold on day 1 and move only as the town does. No conduct has a size of its own.

## Learning what others do: three routes, no fourth

A picture moves only by what reaches a person **along a tie** (what someone known tells them of themselves), **by being there**, or **by what is published**. `Culture.weight` refuses any other route (a test tries them: docs/no-god-view.md). Nobody passes on what a third person did, so one act is never counted twice: a person tells each act once to each person who asks.

| Route | Weight | What moves |
|---|---|---|
| a tie told me of their own act (or said what they think of it) | 1; `close_weight` (2) for a close tie (affinity from `close_affinity_bps`, 3,000) | `does` toward 1 or 0; `approves` toward what they said |
| I was there | 1 | `does` |
| published (a ballot's declared result) | up to `published_weight` (4), as its share | `does` toward the share |

A sighting moves `does` toward it by its weight over the weight held: `does += (x - does) * w / n'`, with `n' = min(window, n + w)`; so the recent counts for more once `window` is full (Cialdini, Reno and Kallgren 1990; adoption that needs several sources and spreads through close ties is complex contagion: Centola and Macy 2007). Approval is the same rule on `approves`, from what others *say* (Bicchieri 2006), weighted `w / (n + w)`.

**What I tell.** Asked how things are, a person adds up to `phrases` (3) of: what they did at an occasion in the last `news_days` ("When a service failed: I wrote to the town."), and what they say of it ("I say writing to the town about a service is wrong."). **Not telling:** I mention my own conduct only if it sits within `conceal_bps` (7,500) of what I take to be approved, or my integrity is high (`integrity_tells_bps`); what was seen cannot be kept back; what I say is `says`. This is why private conduct is under-counted (Kuran 1995).

## Origin

Where people come from, as groups the place's own statistics name (`values.culture.origin`: `groups`, `same_household_bps`, `homophily_bps`, `by_group`), each with its source in the profile; a profile with no figure has no such groups. **Naz, 2026-10-13:** origin divides a town as building, workplace, membership and tenure do. It does two things only:

1. **It makes ties within a group likelier (homophily).** A person tells others which group they come from when asked how things are (only where the profile has `homophily_bps`); when choosing whom to catch up with, and on what a talk adds to a tie, someone of one's own group counts `1 + homophily_bps / 10,000` times (McPherson, Smith-Lovin and Cook 2001; the figure and its source are the profile's, or a marked placeholder).
2. **Where a source gives a group's figures for a conduct (`by_group`), those who come from it start there.**

Nothing in code makes any group better or worse at anything, and no conduct is defined about a group. Prejudice between groups may emerge from the mechanisms (a tie cools toward someone for what they did, and ties are mostly within groups); the platform records it and judges nothing. Origin is generated on a stream of its own (`generator/origin`), so no other draw moves: in each household, whoever has no parent in the town: the first is drawn from the shares, each other is of that group with `same_household_bps`, else drawn again; whoever has parents takes a parent's group (equal chance where the parents differ; no source found gives a split). A child born in a run takes a parent's group at coming of age, and a newcomer is drawn one from the profile's shares: see "Children and newcomers" below.

## Where it is in the decisions (step 3)

One bounded term per conduct, at the draw the conduct already has (`citizen.ts`): `answer_tribunal`, `stay_past_notice` and `take_from_employer` sway the rate before the draw; `take_to_tribunal` (a rent rise challenged, a debt applied for) and `complain` sway it inside `reads`, beside trust and agency; `take_in` sways the affinity a tie must have (a lean toward yes lowers it); `join_union` and `strike` add the lean (times the sway) to the shift beside `union_peers` and the vote's own shift. Where the choice already has an outcome (answer, stay, challenge, take in), the outcome records the numbers and the lean (`norm_does`, `norm_approves`, `norm_own`, `norm_says`, whole percent; `norm_lean`, hundredths of a standard deviation, in its `facts`); join, strike, complain and take had none, so since step 4 each is recorded as a decision of its own (below).

## What a tie's act is to me, what I say, and what it costs (step 4)

**Visible to a tie** means one of two things and no third: the tie **tells it** (once to each who asks, and only if it sits within `conceal_bps` of what they take to be approved, or their integrity is high: what they would rather keep back they keep back, which is why private conduct is under-counted), or I **was there** (step 5). A tie's own act that nobody tells or sees is not known.

**What I make of it, and what I say** (`Culture.react`; `margin_bps` 2,500, `cool_bps` 500, `esteem_bps` 200, `disapproved_bps` 300, placeholders). Told that a tie did (or did not do) a conduct, I judge the act twice, and the two can differ:
- **privately**, by what I think myself (`own`): the act sits with it or against it. Beyond the margin from the middle, **the tie cools** toward them, in proportion, by at most `cool_bps` (what I hold against), or **warms** by at most `esteem_bps` (what I hold with). Nothing is blocked, no one is fined, and a cooler tie is one I catch up with less, as today (Ellickson 1991: neighbours keep order by gossip, coolness and withdrawal; Brennan and Pettit 2004: esteem is what is given and withheld). It is a feeling and it is not said.
- **aloud**, by what I would say (`says`: `own` moved toward what I take to be approved by how much belonging weighs with me and what integrity leaves, the arithmetic of `Inner.view`; Kuran 1995). If that sits beyond the margin I **send a verdict** to the one whose act it was, once, as a typed phrase ("Of your writing to the town about a service: I say it is wrong."). So a person may privately hold an act wrong, cool toward the one who did it, and say aloud that it is right, because they take everyone to say so; and one who privately holds it right may say it is wrong. That is pluralistic ignorance made of the mechanisms: nothing says it must happen, and nothing prevents it.
- **told of what I said:** a conduct a tie told me of is "in the news" for `news_days`, and when asked I add what I say of it (once to each asker, if it is not the middle step): an opinion, never a count of anyone's act, so it counts nobody twice. This is how approval travels between people who did nothing themselves.

**What it costs the one told** (`Culture.judged`): what the verdict says is what is approved, so their `approves` moves toward it along the tie (the same rule as any hearing of approval); held against them, a **dent to belonging** (`Inner.disapproved`), `disapproved_bps` times from a half to one and a half by their conformity ("more for the conformist": Williams 2007, being left out hurts, the more the more one belongs), scaled by how far from the middle the verdict is, heals as any dent does and is remembered ("disapproved" in memory); and their tie to the one who said it cools by half the dent. Approved: no dent, and their tie to the one who said it warms a little. The standing cost is therefore to belonging and to ties, which is what the mind already uses them for.

**A count taken in secret and published breaks it.** A declared ballot's yes share is a published count of how many would act (`does` for strike, as before) and of what those who voted thought: `hearSaid(..., "published")` moves `approves` by up to `published_weight` over the weight held. A street that privately approves and says "wrong" has `approves` low and `own` high (shown in `culture-step4.test.ts`: the gap between mean `own` and mean `approves` is 45 points, thirty rounds of telling each other what they say leave it where it was, and three published readings of "seven in ten would" close it to a few points, after which they say it is right and stop voicing disapproval). A steward's lever is to publish such a count; it costs nothing else (Prentice and Miller 1993).

## Children and newcomers (step 4)

- **A child coming of age** asks the parents it lives with what matters to them (as before). A parent now also answers how they see things here: for each conduct they hold, how many in 10 do it and how many in 10 would call it right, and where they come from. At the same time as their values settle, the child's `does` and `approves` become the **mean of the parents' pictures**, held at `prior_weight` (Cavalli-Sforza and Feldman 1981; Bisin and Verdier 2001: parents first, then everyone else, who are met as sightings from then on), and their own lean restarts from that picture. Their group is a parent's (equal chance where the parents differ; no source found gives a split). A conduct no parent spoke of keeps the picture the child had; where the profile gives a group's figures for it, the child starts there.
- **A newcomer**, anyone made in the run who has no parent here, **arrives holding the profile's picture at `newcomer_weight` (1)**, so what they are told here soon outweighs it (Berry 1997, acculturation), and is of a group drawn on their own stream (`culture-origin`) from the profile's shares where it has groups. Those shares are the town's, not a migrant stream's: the profile gives no other, and a marked placeholder is all this is.
- A child born in the run is also made in the run, so it holds its picture lightly until its parents have answered.

## The lean at the choices that had no record, and the term on a want's way (step 4)

Join (the `membership` decision that existed), the vote on a ballot for action (`strike`), writing to the town (`complain`) and taking from an employer (`take`) now record a **decision** with a `norm` (contracts/dataset.schema.json): the conduct, the four numbers in whole percent and the lean in hundredths of a deviation, beside the options and the chance the choice was made at. The term on a **want's way**: where a way a want may take is a conduct (the union, a desk of the tribunal, the town's desk), the same lean adds `way_points` (1,500, a standard deviation of trust or of agency) times `sway` times the lean to the way's `adj`, bounded as the lean is. The way chosen and the draw that follows are two choices (which door, and whether to go through it), each swayed once.

## Town memory (step 5)

`participants/src/behaviour/townmemory.ts`. What a person carries of what was public: at most `slots` (6) events, each `{kind, about, day, how, hops, strength}`. **Kinds** (closed): `put_out` (a household put out of its home on a warrant), `strike` (a walk-out from work), `ruling`, `law`, `vote` (what the local paper reports of a decision, a law or budget, a declared result), `founded` (a body's statement of purpose, published after the world began). `about` is who did it (the police for a removal; the employer for a walk-out; the maker for a law). The words are the person's and judge nothing ("Talk of the town: a household put out of its home, police, day 45; I hold it at 7 in 10, through 1 hand.").

**What makes an event public, and who witnesses it.** No flag does. An event is public to the extent that people outside the household it happened to hold it, and they come to hold it three ways and no fourth (`orchestrator/test/no-god-view.test.ts`, "town memory"):
1. **They were there, because their own place put them there.** The world shows who left a space, why and when only to those inside the building (`SiteSpace.left`: the last five departures); from outside it shows only that someone was seen leaving (`seen_leaving`), never who or why. A person inside a building at a wake finds the departures there: one with the reason *removed* that is no older than `witness_days` (3) and is not their own household's is **a removal they saw**, taken in once (the departure's space, who and when are its key). **A walk-out is not read off who left a workplace** (tried first, and wrong: a year of the town gave 1,469 walk-outs held and no stoppage in the log, because people leave a workplace at all hours, part-timers among them, and the world shows no cause); the members who stop work hold it as theirs (seen, full strength) and tell it, and the rest of the workplace hears it from them. A person put out on a warrant holds their own removal as seen. This is the presence route step 2 left unwired. Nothing new is added to any channel; no one is told of a removal who was not in the building.
2. **It was published and they read it.** The paper's item (`news`) about a law, a budget, a declared result or a permit decision, for the person who reads the paper (the share the profile gives), held at `read_bps` (5,000) times the item's weight over the heaviest (the paper's own weights); a body's statement of purpose published after the world began, held the same at weight 3.
3. **A tie told them.** Catching up adds one phrase for the strongest event held above `tell_at_bps` (2,000) not yet told to that person (the memory keeps, per event, whom it told). The hearer holds it at `pass_bps` (6,000) of the teller's strength, `negative_bps` (2,000) more if they hold the conduct it shows wrong by their own view (bad news travels further: Bebbington et al. 2017, to confirm), one hand further than the teller; **past `max_hops` (3) it is let go**. Nothing is drawn.

**Holding it.** The weakest gives way to a stronger arrival (by strength now), and a weaker arrival is dropped. Strength halves every 365 days for what was seen and every 180 for what was read or told, **worked out when read** from the days gone; nothing runs daily. Heard again, a held event returns `rehearse_bps` (3,000) of the way to what it first was and nothing else moves. The record's section `heard` is positional, `[kind, about, event day, strength on the day it last arrived (whole percent), how, hands, that day]`, about 30 bytes a slot, written only when something arrives: the fading is the formula's, which anyone reading the record can apply (`rowStrengthPct`), so the days going rewrite nothing and an empty memory writes nothing.

**What it does.** (1) **A sighting of the conduct it shows**: a removal seen is one sighting each of `apply_possession` (the landlord applied; a view held where the world has a law on possession, at the placeholder 5,000 where the profile is silent) and `stay_past_notice` (the household stayed until put out); a walk-out taken part in is not a sighting (it is the person's own act); weight 1, route *here*. This feeds the picture of what others do, which the decision terms read. (2) **Trust in the particular institution, for people it never touched**: the first time an event becomes mine it bears once on trust in whoever did it (the police for a removal, the employer for a walk-out), at its strength times `bystander_bps` (5,000) times how far my own view of the conduct sits from the middle, **whether or not I deal with them**, and **by the sign of my own view**: someone who holds a landlord right to apply trusts the police a little more for it, someone who holds it wrong a little less; someone with no view moves nothing. Hearing it again moves nothing; trust still returns toward where it started by half every two years, so a memory pushes once and fades. The platform has no sign of its own for any event.

**Not built:** commemoration by a body founded after an event (14f); the kinds `let_go_many`, `closed`, `disaster` (nothing public in the world makes them seen yet); a founding read from anywhere but its published purpose.

## What a year showed (2026-10-13; docs/decisions.md, "steps 4 and 5 measured")

1,000 people, three profiles, five seeds each, dial on against off: no standing measure moved that a control does not also move; `take_in` `does` falls 5 to 7 points and nothing else moves by a point except ke-nakuru `complain` `does` (+1.5); `approves` stays at 50; people think and say things 10 points apart on average; a removal seen by 15 people in one building is held by 453 of 1,067 a year later through three hands, and creates trust in the police in about 450 who never dealt with them. The drop condition of 14l is still met for every conduct but `take_in` and, in one profile, `complain`. `scripts/culture-report.ts` reads all of it from a log.

## Why `approves` does not move (2026-10-13; docs/decisions.md, "Culture: why `approves` never moves")

A diagnosis with counters on every route (1,000 and 3,000 people, a year, three profiles): **nothing is unwired, rounded away or misread**; every told act is heard, every hearing is applied, and the record holds what moved to within 0.4 of a point. What keeps `approves` still: (1) what a person *says* is their own view moved toward what they take to be approved by `belonging x (1 - integrity) x 4`, and that factor is **1 for 57% of people** (they say exactly what the street approves; the `4` is the scale of a product of three fractions in `Inner.view`, carried over to a single weight here), so 97 to 99% of what is said is "neither right nor wrong"; (2) a verdict, voiced only if what is *said* is more than 25 points from the middle, is therefore almost never voiced (0 to 13 a town a year); (3) a neutral hearing pulls back toward 50; (4) every person starts at the same figure with a symmetric view, so **the mean of what a town takes to be approved cannot move without a group's own figures or a one-sided event**; only the spread across people does (0 to about 1 point in a year). `culture-report.ts sum`'s drop condition tests the mean, so for `approves` it cannot be met by construction: read `people_sd_end`. `does` does not move for a conduct whose occasion does not come up in a year (the tribunal, a ballot, a complaint: 0 to 4 acts a year in 1,000 people); at 3,000 it moves for the ones that are frequent and visible (ke-nakuru, removals seen: `stay_past_notice` +1.1, `apply_possession` +1.0). The control for gb-eng (five seeds): the union's share of firms moves 0.54 to 1.12 with the dial on and **1.12 again with the norm term at 0**, 0.96 with no conduct at all (groups only): it is the groups and who is told what, not the norm. Not changed: the gap scale (a size; the owner's), the sayings' five steps.

## Ordinary talk about others' conduct (2026-10-13, culture-3)

**What was missing.** Conduct was spoken of only around a rare event: a person told their own act, and said what they thought only of an act they had been told of. Real people talk routinely about what others they know have done (Dunbar, Marriott and Duncan 1997: about two thirds of conversation time is social topics, mostly about the speaker or third parties, from 45 casual conversations; Dunbar 2004; Gluckman 1963; Emler 1994, all to confirm), and that talk is how a street learns what is acceptable and who stands where.

**What it is, on the talks people already have.** Nothing new runs daily and there is no new loop. When a person is asked how things are (the catch-up that already exists), they may add one phrase telling of **one other person's act that they know first-hand**, with what they *say* of it (the same `says` as everywhere, so a person who falsifies aloud falsifies here): `About p12: When a service failed: they wrote to the town. Of that, I say it is wrong.` The hearer takes it by the rules that already exist and no new ones:
- one more sighting of the conduct along a tie (weight 1, or 2 if the teller is a close tie): it moves `does`;
- what the teller said is what is approved (`hearSaid`), as a verdict is: it moves `approves`;
- they may say what they think of the matter themselves when asked later (`noteHeard`);
- **the one talked about gains or loses standing among those who heard:** by the hearer's own view (`react`, step 4), the hearer's tie to them cools or warms, if they are anyone to the hearer (an existing tie). A stranger's act moves the hearer's picture and creates no tie. The one talked about is not told, and takes no dent from this.

**Nobody has a god's view.** A teller tells only of someone **who told them** of their own act (so a tie of theirs: `learnOf`, called only from the first-person phrase heard from that person), never of the asker, each act once to each asker, for `gossip_days` after, and of at most `third_kept` people. What they tell is not re-told: a hearer does not keep it to tell on, so an act travels one hand and is never an echo of an echo. The same act reaching a hearer a second time, or one they already had first-hand from the person, is not counted again. A person cannot be told of from a register, a directory or a count: `hearAbout` takes only a tie's route. Held in `orchestrator/test/no-god-view.test.ts` ("ordinary talk adds no channel...") and `participants/test/culture-talk.test.ts`. *Not built:* telling of what the teller *saw* (presence) or *read* (publication): the sources of town memory are a separate mechanism, and an act seen is not tied to a named person in the world's views.

**The sizes, all placeholders, fixed before any run and not adjusted after seeing results:**

| Size | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `gossip_bps` | 3,000 | the chance a talk carries one such tale if the teller has one; of the order of the share of talk time that is about absent others (Dunbar et al. 1997: about two thirds is social topics, most of it about the speaker or third parties; the split inside it was not in what was opened), and kept low because the conducts here are a short list, not all talk about third parties |
| `gossip_days` | 180 | the span over which an act is still news about a person one knows; same order as a tie's half-life (180 days) |
| `third_kept` | 8 | the people whose acts one keeps to tell of; well inside the ~15-person sympathy group and the `max_contacts` a person keeps (Hill and Dunbar 2003) |

The chance is one draw on the person's own stream (`culture-gossip`), made only when there is an act to tell. **What the supply is:** a person can tell only of acts of conducts that occasions produce, so in a town where an occasion (a tribunal case, a failed service) is rare, there is little to tell; ordinary talk does not invent acts.

## Cost

Nothing runs daily. A norm moves at a catch-up, a sighting or a published count; fading is worked out when a figure is read, and the section is worked out again only when a picture has moved or a month has gone (an unchanged section is the same array, not compared again, not written again). A decision costs one term, about ten integer operations. Measured (docs/decisions.md, 2026-10-13, "Culture: the cost of carrying the section"): culture's own computing (hearing, telling, the terms) is about 0.25% of a run's time. The kernel's record writes are 2.9% of a 60-day run of the 1,000-person town (630 ms of 22 s), and carrying the section adds to those writes 16% as named rows (+0.45% of the run) and 7% as positional rows (+0.19%); the log's growth is 0.4%. The 2.3% to 2.8% first reported was a whole-run timing, which on a shared machine varies by about 4% between identical runs, and did not reproduce. **The floor** is the kernel's: a record write canonicalises the merged record twice and hashes it, whichever section moved, so every section is paid for by its size at every write (630 ms with no culture at all). Only a change to the kernel (keeping each section's canonical bytes, so a write pays for what it changed) would make an unchanged section free; it is not made here (the kernel is not this work's), and it would make every section of every record cheaper, not only this one.
