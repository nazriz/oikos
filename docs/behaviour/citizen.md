# `citizen.default` (v0.49.0): the AI citizen in code

**Seat:** a townsperson (acts as themselves). **Source:** `participants/src/behaviour/citizen.ts`, with its mind in `participants/src/behaviour/mind.ts`, the richer record in `participants/src/behaviour/inner.ts` and the sparse mind in `participants/src/llm/consult.ts`. **Design:** [docs/design/ai-citizens.md](../design/ai-citizens.md). **Citations:** [docs/research/references.md](../research/references.md).

It wakes weekly, whenever something names it (an offer, a notice, a message), and when a gathering it means to go to starts or ends.

**Everything happens the way a person does it.** They apply, sign, move in, ask their bank to pay, write, turn up. Nothing happens to them automatically, and they know only what they've seen or been told.

**Households** ([households.md](households.md)): a citizen lives in a household. The rules on homes and leaving town are the **leaseholder's**, for the household: the budget is a third of household income, the search is for the bedrooms the occupancy standard asks for, and the whole household moves and leaves together. Other adults follow the leaseholder and pay a share of the rent. Children act on their own only from 18, when they open a bank account. Voice (petitions) and complaints are the leaseholders'.

**Work by the day** (0.42.1; [firms.md](firms.md), "Work by the day"): a job whose pay is a day's. Its holder takes the day's pay (whole, or less what the law takes from a day's pay) as their wages; counts a month's income as the working days of a month; and, a pay period and five days without pay, ends it without notice and without a claim (nothing is owed for a day not worked), and looks for other work. A job an employer does not register ([firms.md](firms.md)) looks to its holder like any job that pays the whole wage.

**Taking up support** (the Rust port, lane A1, 2026-10-17; risks A-30): where the world's profile states a take-up share (`take_up_bps`), a person entitled to a payment claims it with that chance, decided once for each payment in each spell of entitlement on a draw keyed to the person and the payment (so a town and its twin claim alike). Real take-up is well under full: about six in ten of those entitled to Pension Credit claimed it in Great Britain (DWP, "Income-related benefits: estimates of take-up", FYE 2018: about 60% by caseload, 70% by expenditure), and the OECD's review found 40 to 80% for social assistance and housing benefits (Hernanz, Malherbet and Pellizzari 2004, to confirm). No share given: everyone entitled claims, as before; no profile states one yet.

**A tenant's death** (the Rust port, lane A1; the bug hunt of 2026-10-17, finding 3): when the household's leaseholder dies, whoever of the household sees it reports the death to the registry (naming the landlord where the household knows it, so the registry's entry reaches it), and the survivor still living there is not homeless: the landlord's practice (firm.landlord 0.20.0, [firms.md](firms.md)) offers the household member the tenancy, and the survivor signs it if the rent is one they can bear, staying where they are, the rent paid from when the old lease ends. Until then a partner who had not held the lease began hunting for a home the day the leaseholder died, and the dead tenant was chased for arrears and evicted through the tribunal.

**Working for oneself** (0.43.0; [entry.md](entry.md), "A person working for themself"): someone the world starts in a business of their own (`own_work`) runs it in its one position and, as themself, is not out of work: they read no job board, apply for nothing and claim no payment for being out of work. Their income is what the business has paid them over the days income is reckoned on (before that, what it was expected to bring). In working hours they are at their pitch, if they trade from one. Nobody takes such work up or gives it up during a run: that waits for the citizen redesign.

## Who a citizen is, and how it decides (the mind)

| | What | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Values** | Schwartz's ten: what a person believes in. | **Temperament** | From values:<br>• openness (stimulation, self-direction): exploring;<br>• conformity (conformity, tradition): copying what most do;<br>• prestige (achievement, power): copying the respected. | Dwarf Fortress's facets beside values; Boyd and Richerson's conformist bias; Rogers |

| **Asking the model** | The sparse mind, **off unless a run turns it on** (`--consult`). Deliberating about joining the union at a new job, or starting a business with premises in reach, a citizen asks once how much each option would matter to someone like it. It decides at the next wake, its own draw from the answer's spread shifting the cited rate (`sway.consult`). Answers are remembered for each kind of person and thing, and logged. | docs/design/ai-citizens.md section 4 (after Affordable Generative Agents, AgentTorch, GenWorld) |
| **Recorded** | Every decision the mind makes goes with the citizen's next action: the area, the way, the choice, each option's score, and what the citizen had before it (each option's facts, how many they know took it, what it means to them, what they have learned of it; their routine; their circumstances; who they are, once). Full logs keep it as a `decision` annotation (`--record-decisions none` leaves them out), exported as `citizen_decisions.jsonl`. And how each choice turned out, when and as the citizen comes to know it: every time the mind evaluates how something went (a shop's delivery, a wage paid or not, who came to a gathering, what a friend said of theirs) the facts and the mind's own figures (the payoff, what it had learned of the option before and after, the feeling) go with the next action as an `outcome` annotation, naming the decision it follows; exported under each decision and as `citizen_outcomes.jsonl`. Recording changes nothing a citizen does. | docs/design/ai-citizens.md section 7 |

## The record (0.44.0; docs/design/ai-citizens.md 10c, 10d)

**What it is.** One record a person, held by the kernel as state and written only by it, on the word of the platform's bookkeeping for that person, named in the world's config (`record_keeper`; for a citizen run by code, its own module) and of nothing a model can reach: the participant API has no act for it, and a model's reply sets nothing. The citizen's mind computes each change (arithmetic in `inner.ts`; no model anywhere) and the change goes to the kernel with the citizen's next action, at most once a wake: a week's plan, or something that happened. An organisation's record is kept the same way, by the bookkeeping of the one post the world names for it (`keeps_record`). The kernel bounds it (8,192 bytes unless the world's `caps.entity_record_bytes` says otherwise; a person's is about 1 to 2 KB), chains a hash of its changes, and shows it to nobody but the person. `oikos record <log> <person>` rebuilds one from a log and checks every hash. A section is written again only when it has moved by more than its grain (trust by 1 point in 100, ties by 5, attachments and views by 2), so the record is a summary to read, not the mind's working figures. Its shape, every list bounded: `contracts/entity-record.schema.json`.

**What it holds:** values, temperament, needs, sense of agency, trust (in up to 12 particular institutions, and in people), attachments (place, trade, group), views (of up to 4 who hold power over the person: thought and said), ties (the 5 closest and the 3 most resented), beliefs (up to 6), wants (up to 6: "Wants and initiative", below; empty for someone who takes no initiative), memory (the last 8 things that happened and 4 that defined the person, kept for good), where the last week's hours went, and circumstances.

**Read by behaviour:** belonging and meaning count in how satisfied a person is (which decides how they decide); what is said of the town is said; values that have moved weigh needs and sway rates as values always did. Trust, agency and attachments are read by what a person does only if they take initiative ("Wants and initiative" and "Readers", below); for everyone else, and in a town with the dial at 0, they are recorded and moved and read by no decision. A reading of a run must say which.

**Where the hours go.** At each wake the time since the last is counted to what the person was at, by where their body is: `work` (their workplace, or their pitch), `learning` (the school), `gathering`, `care` (the clinic's waiting room), `home`, `away` (anywhere else, or nowhere). A week's total goes into the record. The way there is not told apart, and nobody's time at home is divided into housework, care of others and leisure: the mind does not know it. The categories sit inside the major divisions of the United Nations' classification of activities for time-use statistics (ICATUS 2016, to confirm), which has nine; these are six.

**Parameters** (`inner`; every figure a placeholder unless the tables above name a source): `trust` (`start_bps` 5000, `spread_bps` 1500, `values_bps` 500, `good_bps` 300, `bad_bps` 600, `heard_bps` 5000, `return_half_life_days` 730, `kept` 12), `agency` (`mean_bps` 5000, `sd_bps` 2000, `worked_bps` 300, `failed_bps` 300, `done_to_bps` 600, `return_half_life_days` 365), `needs` (`dent_half_life_days` 180, `dents`), `values` (`stability_bps` 6000, `stability_days` 1278, `shock_bps` 400, `shocks`), `views` (`integrity_bps` 5000, `integrity_values_bps` 1500, `kept` 4), `attach` (`place_half_life_days` 1825, `trade_half_life_days` 1095, `group_half_life_days` 365, `fade_half_life_days` 365), `memory` (`recent` 8, `defining` 4, `defining_bps` 5000). The world's: `outlook` (the profile's), `own_values` (each person's), `institutions` (which office is which kind of institution, where a person does not know it by a parameter of their own).

### Values move

- **Slow drift.** Each month every value takes a small random step and is pulled a little toward the town's mean: sized so that a person's values after three and a half years correlate with their own before at 0.6, and so that the spread of values across the town stays what the profile says. Source: rank-order stability of 0.48 to 0.69 over 3.5 years in two German panels (docs/research/references.md, "Values that move"); 0.6 taken. Measured stability includes measurement error, so real values move less: this is an upper bound on drift. The step is drawn from the person's stream for that month (the kernel's keyed streams).
- **Shocks**, at once, 400 points a value at full closeness (a placeholder: about a quarter of a standard deviation):

| Shock | When | Values moved | Source for the direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| `bereaved` | a death in the household (closeness 1), or told of the death of someone one is tied to (closeness: the affinity) | security, tradition, benevolence up; stimulation, hedonism down | Threat raises security and tradition and lowers stimulation (Sortheix et al. 2019; Verkasalo et al. 2006; Daniel et al. 2013); the widowed grow more religious (Brown et al. 2004). **No study of values after a bereavement was found: by analogy** |
| `child` | the one who bears a child, from the pregnancy | security, conformity, tradition up; stimulation, hedonism down | Mothers' conservation values rise from early pregnancy to after the birth, fathers' do not (Lönnqvist, Leikas and Verkasalo 2018, to confirm) |
| `ruined` | nothing left to try for making ends meet (the goal given up) | security, tradition, conformity, benevolence up; hedonism, self-direction, stimulation down | Young Europeans after the financial crisis (Sortheix et al. 2019) |
| `evicted` | notice from a landlord to leave a home; leaving it on a notice, or put out of it on a warrant | security up; self-direction down | By analogy with threat: placeholder |
| `left_on_order` | leaving a home on the tribunal's possession order, with no warrant carried out on it | as `evicted` (the same weights: `LIKE` in `inner.ts`) | A kind of its own on the record so that a person who went on an order is not told as one put out by officers (`put_out` is the warrant's own); weighs as being made to leave. Added 2026-10-13 |

- What a need weighs follows the values as they now are; temperament does not move.
- **Not built:** a shock fading back (after 11 September security rose and fell back within months: Verkasalo et al. 2006; here the monthly pull toward the town's mean takes a shock back slowly); a conversion; values moving toward those of the people one is tied to.

### Upbringing

- **In a town as it is made,** every person's values are the town's content, as beliefs at the start are. An adult's are drawn from the profile. Someone living with their parents (a child of any age) has, for each value, the town's mean, plus half of how far their parents' mean sits from it, plus a part of their own sized so that the spread of values among children is the profile's. So households hold together a little, and the town's rates do not depend on who was whose child.
- **Someone born in a run, or who came as a child,** has values drawn for them until they come of age. Then they ask the parents they live with what matters to them, and each answers in words: the three values they hold most and the three least. A fortnight on, the asker's values settle toward each parent's most and away from their least, by the same half. Nothing is asked of anyone before that.
- **The half** (`UPBRINGING.share_bps` 5000) is a **placeholder**. Parents' and children's values are alike; a typical correlation of 0.2 to 0.3 is recalled and was not confirmed (Knafo and Schwartz 2003; Schönpflug 2001; Barni et al. 2014: much of the likeness is to the country's norm, not the family's). Twin studies put the heritable share of values anywhere from a quarter to most (Twito and Knafo-Noam 2020).
- **Not built:** those a child grows up among beyond their parents (the town's mean stands for them); a newcomer's children (drawn afresh).

### Relationships

- **A tie** is someone known: how close (affinity, which may be below nothing: resentment), how far trusted, and what last passed between us and when (talked, met, a tip, mourned; unpaid, let go, a rent rise, a notice). Trust in a tie starts at the profile's share who say most people can be trusted, rises a little each time they answer and a good deal when a tip of theirs turns out so.
- **Bounded.** A person keeps as many as they keep contacts (`max_contacts` 15, the sympathy group: Hill and Dunbar 2003) and three more; the faintest gives way. The record holds the five closest (the support clique of about five: Dunbar and Spoors 1995, to confirm; people name about two to three they discuss important matters with: McPherson, Smith-Lovin and Brashears 2006) and the three most resented, who may be organisations: a landlord, an employer.
- **How a loss becomes a circle's.** Whoever of a household sees a death at home tells those they are closest to outside it (one letter). A person told of the death of someone they were tied to is bereaved by how close they were; either way the one who told them is closer for it, and the dead are no longer among those they turn to. A person let go, or given notice to leave their home, says so when asked how things are, for `news_days`; those who hear it and deal with the same employer or landlord trust them less.
- **Not built:** the social moves (help, snub, gossip); someone tied to the dead whom no survivor knows (they find out when nobody answers); a death outside a home; sympathy as a feeling of its own.

## Wants and initiative (0.45.0; docs/design/ai-citizens.md 10a, 10b)

**Source:** `participants/src/behaviour/wants.ts` (what a want is, what an opening brings, how they meet), the citizen's `waysFor`, `pursue` and `wantBorn`. **Only for someone who takes initiative:** each person draws once, from their own stream, against the world's dial (`dials.initiative_bps`; a world may also say it of a person, `initiative`). With the dial absent or at 0 nobody does, no want is held, nothing below is read, and the town is the one it was before (held to the state hash by `orchestrator/test/living-dial.test.ts`).

**A want** is something the person is trying to bring about. The record holds up to 6, heaviest first:

| | |
|---|---|
| `about` | what it is about, in a word: a need (`security`, `shelter`, `social`, `status`, `belonging`, `meaning`), or what happened (`rent_increase`, `notice`, `let_go`, `unpaid`, `loss`, `slight`, `service`) |
| `whom` | whom it concerns, where it is about someone: a person, an organisation or a post (this landlord, this employer, the town; someone lost). A want may seek nothing typed and be about someone only: a grievance |
| `seeks` | what would satisfy it, as typed outcomes of the blocks' vocabulary (docs/design/ai-citizens.md 2c) seen from the person: money to me, money from me (less of it), occupancy, work, attend, goods. Any one would |
| `against` | the paper in its way: which, of what kind, who made it (a notice of rent increase, a notice to leave) |
| `weight`, `since`, `from` | how much it matters; since when; born of a need, of an event, or of what someone the person copies is after |
| `with` | the household it is held with (below) |
| `tried`, `way` | how often something was tried, and the way last taken |

Sizes are placeholders (`event_bps`) |
| **Those one copies** | Asked how things are, a person says what they are after and by what way ("What I'm after: security by offer:employment."), and what they have stopped trying for. Someone short of the same need (below their aspiration) takes the want up once `copy_from` (2; the conformist 1) of those they know are after it; it is met when their life is back to what they aspire to, and is not taken up from others again for `rest_days`. Ways others took count as others having gone that way. | Conformist transmission (Boyd and Richerson 1985); 3b's imitation |

**A household's want.** What befalls a household is told by the member it happened to, to its other adults, in the household's own phrasing ("Trouble for us: landlord-3 is raising our rent."). Each who hears holds the want in their own record, naming the household (`with`), at their own weight (half the event's size, as their own values weigh it), and it dents their belonging and meaning as a loss at half closeness does: a member's loss reaches the others as more than income. Each goes after it by what they can see (the other adult looks for better pay; the leaseholder asks the landlord). Source: a spouse's wellbeing falls almost as far as the unemployed person's (Marcus 2013); household strategies (docs/research/references.md, to confirm).

**How a want meets an opening: no list of verbs.** An opening is anything the person can see, reduced to what taking it up would bring, in the same typed terms (`bringsOf` reads an offer's commitments):

| Opening | What it brings or bears on | Taken up by |
|---|---|---|
| The job board (`offer:employment`) | what its offers' terms say: money to the taker, and work (to someone in work, money only: work they have) | looking, and applying (the mind's choice of employer, as before); in work, only for better pay |
| The homes board (`offer:lease`) | a home; housed, one that asks less; and being rid of the landlord | hunting while housed: a lease offered is signed, the old home left at once and its rent paid until its tenancy ends (notice given if none runs) |

A want and an opening meet when the opening brings an outcome the want seeks, can undo the paper in its way, or bears on whom it concerns (`meets`). The acts are those of taking up, one per kind of opening (the contract's own `take_up`, a letter, an application): none is listed per want. A grievance that nothing the person can see bears on meets nothing: it sits in the record, grows or is given up, and is said (below).

**Which way, or none, is the mind's.** Among the ways that meet the want, and waiting, the choice is by the logit of what the mind has learned of each kind of way (a new learning area, `way`: experience-weighted attraction, as for shops and employers; an untried way is expected to give `expect` 5,000) plus, for each (`choice`; every size a placeholder):
- **trust** in the institution or party, against the town's figure for its kind (`trust` 1,500 a standard deviation): the trusting turn to the tribunal, the town, the landlord; the wary do not;
- **fear** of someone they depend on counts against asking them or signing against them (`fear`);
- **sense of agency** counts for acting at all and against waiting (`agency` 1,500 a standard deviation);
- **belief** in what a host teaches counts for turning to it (`belief`);
- **those they know** who took that way (`others`, weighed again by conformity).
Each choice is a recorded decision (area `want`); how the way turned out is learned (met: 10,000; granted or refused when asked: 10,000 or 1,000; not yet: 2,000) and moves the sense of agency as anything tried does.
Since 0.49.0 each such decision, and each draw of whether to found, is recorded with its situation (the want, what stands behind it, every opening in typed terms with what the person knows of it, their own record, the odds the mind gave): what a model giving the odds in the mind's place would be shown (docs/design/ai-citizens.md 11a). Recording only: nothing reads it back.

**When.** A want is gone after when it is born of an event (the wake the event caused), and again each time its way has had its time: `patience_days` (45) stretched by the person's patience (tradition, security, attachment to the place). At most two a week; most weeks, none: people still repeat. Nothing is added to the scheduler: a person wakes weekly and when something names them, as before.

**Unmet for long.** After `tries` (3) the want grows (`grow_bps` a quarter heavier, at most `grows` twice) in someone persistent (achievement, self-direction and a sense of agency above the town's), and is given up by the rest; given up, it is not born again for `rest_days` (180). Giving up on making ends meet is ruin, as before. **Giving a want up does not stop a routine:** someone out of work who has given up on work still applies each week, as the rule for those in the labour force has it (leaving the labour force is not yet a person's decision: "Stopping work"). **Withdrawal** (waiting when nothing seen would answer it, or with a sense of agency a standard deviation below the town's) is recorded as an outcome (`withdrew`), and said when asked how things are ("I've stopped trying about notice: there's no use."). Source: learned helplessness as the default that control has to be learned against (Maier and Seligman 2016); discouraged job seekers are about 3 per 1,000 people of working age in the first profile's country (ABS, *Potential workers*, February 2026: 64,000 of 22.4 million; opened).

**Met** when what it sought has come about: the need back above its level by `met_margin_bps`; in work again; the notice of increase withdrawn, or the landlord left; housed elsewhere or let stay; the debt paid; the dents of a loss healed; the waste collected.

**For those who take initiative, wants stand where the two goals did** ("making ends meet", "more company": "Goals", above). Their method lists were a list of verbs per goal; the same hooks (spending less, a cheaper home, better work, catching up, trying a gathering) are now reached by whichever opening the want met.

## Things that go wrong, and what follows (0.46.0)

What goes wrong for a person, how often, and what they can do about it is one page: [frictions.md](frictions.md). In a town with the dial for it on, a person sees a home fallen into disrepair, pay that comes short, a bill well over the usual, a refused claim, goods that are faulty; each is a grievance about whoever did it and, for someone who takes initiative, a want. Four things changed beside it, each for those who take initiative unless said:

- **Moving to another job.** Before, any posting that paid more than the job held was worth taking, and about a fifth of the employed changed employer in a year against the 7.7 to 9.6% the place's survey gives. Now another job must pay `job_move.premium_bps` more, and `per_year_bps` more again for each year in the job held (up to `cap_years`; the years before the world began count for the job a person began with: `started.trade_days`). The profile gives the three figures, fitted to the survey's rate (docs/decisions.md, "Everyday frictions"); with none given, as before. The basis for the shape: those who move gain about a tenth in pay over those who stay, and the chance of leaving falls steeply with tenure (docs/research/references.md; both to confirm).
- **Giving up on work stops the looking.** A want for work that is given up (about being let go, being unpaid, or work itself) stops the weekly look at the job board and the applications, for as long as a want given up rests (`wants.rest_days`, 180). The person notes it for themselves (`work_search`, seen by nobody else), and the economy's summary counts them out of the labour force from then until they look again or are in work, as a labour force survey counts someone not looking (measure/queries/economy/summary.sql). They still claim what they are entitled to.
- **A need short at the start has been short for a while.** A need found below its level in a town's first week is taken to have been so for a part of the 60 days it takes to become a want, drawn for the person. Before, every clock started at day 0 and nobody wanted anything for two months of any run (the first living pilot: 73 wants in 60 days, nearly all landlords').
- **What is told to the household is said once, until it changes** (everyone): a member's income is told to the leaseholder when the sum as it is said changes. One household in the first living pilot was sent "My pay is now 0.00" 478 times in 60 days: the figures compared were not the figures said.
  The same rule holds for the leaseholder's message of each member's share of the rent (kept by recipient, `sharesTold`): said again only when the share changes. Read by sender and recipient, the second living pilot's 1,754 "My pay is now" (938 pairs) and 1,003 "Your share of the rent" (896 pairs) had no unchanged repeat; they were each member's first telling (137 at day 0) and real changes (about 410 wage raises of 2%, jobs lost and found, rent and earner counts changing). `orchestrator/test/household-saying.test.ts` holds it over a run.

## Founding, and what is founded (0.47.0)

One page: [founding.md](founding.md). In a town with the dial for it on (`living.founding`), a person who takes initiative and gives a want up, or can see nothing that would answer it, and who knows at least two others in the same case, may start something of their own: a group that gathers at their home, a fund its members pay into and draw on, a body that takes its members' grievances up, or (by the existing way) a business. Whether and what shape are two draws on the person's own stream, from who they are and what the want is about. What they found is an organisation with them in its first post (`group.founded`), which says what it is for, puts up a gathering and offers membership.

- **Others' foundings are openings like any other.** A standing offer of membership (a public statement of kind `membership`) is read for what its terms bring in typed outcomes and, where its offerer says it takes up matters like ours for its members, whom that bears on; the mind chooses it among the ways for a want, or not. Joining is an application, the membership agreement offered, signed, and dues by standing payment where it asks any.
- **Leaving.** Someone who has not come for `founding.leave_after_days` gives notice and stops their dues; so does every member of a thing that winds up.
- **A fund.** A member short of money to fall back on asks for the draw its terms state, at most once a month; the fund pays or says why not.
- **Eligibility by dealing.** An offer or a gathering may be for those party to an agreement in force with someone (`dealing_with`: their tenants, their workers), which a person checks against their own agreements.
- **Who shares a want** is only what the person knows: their household, what those they caught up with said, who told them they were wronged, who mourns the same death, who lives in the same building.
- With the dial off none of it is read, and the town is step 2b's to the state hash.

## Pay, a home lost for arrears, and a business of one (0.48.0, 2026-10-11)

Three changes on the person's side of the firm and landlord fixes (docs/decisions.md, "Four faults on the firm and landlord side"); each is described where its mechanism lives.

- **What counts as pay.** Money from an employer's account is pay only if the bank's line says it was paid as wages. A refund from the shop one works at, or a dividend from the works one holds a share of, is not pay come short (docs/behaviour/firms.md, "Wages owed").
- **Told to leave for arrears** (docs/behaviour/justice.md, "Possession"; only where the world has a law on possession). A notice to vacate: gone by its date, or staying, by a draw of the person's own at the world's share (`possession.stay_bps`). An application for possession: the tenant answers the tribunal with what the household could pay a month toward the arrears (its income less rent and essentials), if anything. A plan: they stay. An order for possession: gone by its date or not (`possession.obey_bps`). A warrant carried out: out, from that hour. Then as anyone without a home. The record keeps it as an eviction.
- **A business of one** (docs/behaviour/entry.md, "Starting a business of one"): started with the month's chance by someone with a home to trade from and the savings (after notice, if in a job); given up after months in which it has paid them under half of essentials.

## Told to leave, asked for rent, written to (0.50.0, 2026-10-12)

- **A notice to vacate does what a notice on the lease did**, once a tenancy: trust in the landlord falls at double weight, the tie falls by 3,000, fear of them rises by 5,000, the household's other adults are told, and (with initiative) the want about it is born, which is met when the notice lapses, the tribunal dismisses or sets a plan, or the household is housed elsewhere. Under a law on possession the lease's own notice comes only after an order, so until this version a tenant told to go felt none of it, from a seat's landlord or a code one. Being put out, or leaving at an order's date, is still the defining event; the notice is not.
- **On the record and nothing more:** a landlord's request for rent owed (`rent_asked`), an application for possession, when the notice of hearing comes (`possession_applied`), and an offer of a plan (`plan_offered`), which is answered with what the household could pay a month beyond its rent and essentials. Whether these should move trust or agency has no source here, so they do not.
- **A letter from an office in its own words** is read, kept and noted (docs/behaviour/letters.md, "A letter to a person").

## Answering the tribunal, and losing a home (0.53.0, 2026-10-12)

- **Served with an application for possession**, a tenant answers or does not: one draw for each application at the profile's rate of tenants who answer (`possession.answer_bps`), read by trust in courts, the sense of agency, what a desk or tribunal has been found to do, fear of the landlord and whether there is somewhere to go. What is told is typed and the tenant's own; what is left out follows the gap between what they think of the landlord and what they say (docs/behaviour/justice.md, "A tenant's answer").
- **The order and the removal are on the record** as events of their own (`ordered_out`, `put_out`), each about whoever did it, and move trust in the tribunal and the police. **So is leaving on the order** (`left_on_order`, 2026-10-13): a household that goes at the order's date with no warrant carried out on the home. It was recorded as `evicted`, as was a removal, so an exit account said "evicted us" of people nobody had put out. `evicted` stays for a notice and for a removal; records written before this keep the old word and read as before.
- **With no dwelling** the leaseholder asks the people the household is closest to whether it may stay, and is taken in where there is room, means and willingness; asked, a person answers by the same rule (docs/behaviour/households.md, "Taken in during the year"). Parameters: `stay` (`ask`, `ask_again_days`, `willing_affinity_bps`: placeholders).
- **Draws about a notice and an order** (`stay-`, `obey-`, `answer-`, `tells-`) are keyed on the lease, never on a paper's number, so a run and its twin draw the same for the same tenancy.

## Attention: what a citizen looks at (D43)

A citizen doesn't receive the housing or job market. It looks when it needs to, the way a person reads the listings when hunting:
- **Needing a home:** standing rental listings within its budget and with the bedrooms needed, dearest first, the top 20.
- **Needing work, or looking for better:** standing job postings, best-paid first, the top 20.
- **Every wake:** a glance at the petitions board. Signature counts arrive as an ordinary report.
- **Every plan, in work** (0.36.0): the enterprise agreements at its own workplace made since it started there (the same look as the boards; before, every workplace's agreement was shown to everyone).
- **Always:** its own records (among them its own titles), money and holdings, the laws, the town's gatherings and teachings, and prices.
- **The land register** (0.36.0): never shown, searched when a paper of its own calls for it: the plan of a lot it holds, and a title passed to it with the dealings in it ([ownership.md](ownership.md), "What an owner knows").

## What a person remembers of public goings-on (0.59.0, 2026-10-24; risk SC-1)

What is published as it comes (the paper, notices of gatherings, what groups teach, petitions' counts, what joining a
group asks, an election's papers) reaches every reader, and until 0.59.0 each person kept and weighed all of it for good:
a town's memory grew as people times papers, 2.8 GB a simulated day at 50,000 people. Now a person keeps a bounded slice
(`citizen/memory.rs`, the world's `public_memory`; the generator's `PUBLIC_MEMORY`):

- **What they saw lately:** everything put out in the last `days` (30).
- **What touched them, whatever its age:** their own papers; what was addressed to them; what answers a paper of theirs
  (a petition's count, a response); what speaks for someone they have dealings with (an employer, a landlord, a group
  they joined, the union: a party to an agreement of theirs in force); and what their mind holds to (a gathering they
  go to or are at, a teaching they believe).
- **Of the rest still standing, the most recent `most` (20) of each kind.** A withdrawn paper older than the month goes.

The rest is let go once a week (`every_days`, 7), as if never seen: the book no longer answers it (`Book::forget`), and
what the mind kept about it goes with it (read marks, the decision to go to a gathering, what was learned of it). Nothing
else is let go: the law, the forms, the budget, agreements, claims, letters and anything not public. A paper comes only
once, so nothing let go returns as news. The sizes are placeholders, a bound and not a fit: people attend to and
remember a bounded, recent, relevant slice of public news (limited attention, Simon; forgetting, Ebbinghaus: to
confirm, references.md). A spec's `public_memory: false` keeps everything, as before.

A citizen sees the lots touching where it lives, works and gathers ("Around you"): what's on each, and how full it is. In its own buildings it sees who's there. The view moves when it moves. It also tells whether it's actually in its workplace before leaving it.

## Stopping work (0.41.0)

**The rule.** A person in the labour force has an age at which they stop work. At it they give notice and never look for work again. Three ways a world says the age:
- `retirement_age`: one age for everyone (how every town was until 2026-10-08);
- `stops_work_at`: this person's own age, or `"never"`, given when the world was made;
- `stop_work_curve`: the share of people in the labour force by age band, from which someone who was given no age (a newcomer, a child born here) draws theirs, once, from their own randomness.

**From a curve to an age** (`stopsWorkAt`, `participants/src/behaviour/payments.ts`). The curve is the place's labour force survey: bands in order of age, the first the reference (the ages before leaving begins). Of those in the labour force at the reference ages, the share still in it in a later band is that band's share over the reference's. A person is given one number and stops at the start of the first band whose share of the reference is at or below it; someone whose number is under the last band's share never stops. So if 87% are in at 25 to 54 and 61% at 60 to 64, three in ten of those in at 54 have stopped by 60. A share that rises again with age brings nobody back. This is how the age of leaving the labour market is read from participation by age (OECD, the effective age of labour market exit; to confirm).

**What it is not.** The pension's age is the payment's own (the support law). Someone past it may work and draw; someone who stopped before it has no pension until it, and lives on savings and on what others in the household earn (a household with nobody earning and no money left leaves town, by the rule that was already there). Whether a person is in the labour force at all is still the profile's one participation figure; the curve says only how that share falls with age.

**Limits, to be written into any reading of a run.**
- The age is drawn once and never moves. A steward who changes the pension's age changes who is paid, not who works. That the pension's terms move the age people stop (Gruber and Wise 1999; Stock and Wise 1990; to confirm) is a person weighing an offer's terms: it belongs to the citizen redesign (docs/design/ai-citizens.md).
- People stop at the start of a band, on a birthday, not spread through it.
- Where the survey's last band is open (65 and over), nobody is drawn to stop after its start: the share working at those ages is right in all, and too old on average.
- Nobody enters the labour force with age (a share lower at 20 than at 40 is not used).

## Leaves out (for now)

- Voice beyond petitions, complaints and elections: protest, contacting councillors, campaigning for a candidate.
- Knowing which laws already exist (a citizen may petition for a law already passed).
- Buying homes; commuting; housing quality beyond price and bedrooms; renegotiating rent.
- Moving house while housed: looking for somewhere cheaper or better while keeping the home, and giving notice once a new lease is signed. A household hunts only once it's without a home, so a rent it can't bear means notice first, then the hunt (the realistic order is the other way round).
- The other social moves: help, snub, gossip, reputation.
- Goals with sub-goals; learning which methods work.
- Misremembering. (Memory is bounded since 0.44.0: the last eight things and four that defined the person.)
- Founding more than once; taking over what someone else founded; a founded thing acting beyond gathering, a fund and a hearing (a picket, a petition of its own, a rule for its members).
- The sparse mind on a real model: its robustness audit (`oikos consult-audit`) is built and tested on the mock, not yet run on one.

## Cut hours (0.56.0, 2026-10-13)

An employer's paper cutting or restoring our hours (`hours_notice`, `hours_restored`) is the pay agreed from the day it names, so a cut we were told of or agreed to is not pay come short, and our income, rent, essentials and offers follow it. Asked to agree to a cut, we answer by a paper (`hours_reply`) with the world's share who agree (`consent_bps`, a placeholder) on our own draw (`hours-consent-<agreement>-<day>`). Pay lower than the whole with no paper behind it is short pay, as before (frictions.md). See firms.md, "Hours before heads".
