# `citizen.default` (v0.33.0): the AI citizen in code

**Seat:** a townsperson (acts as themselves). **Source:** `participants/src/behaviour/citizen.ts`, with its mind in `participants/src/behaviour/mind.ts` and the sparse mind in `participants/src/llm/consult.ts`. **Design:** [docs/design/ai-citizens.md](../design/ai-citizens.md). **Citations:** [docs/research/references.md](../research/references.md).

It wakes weekly, whenever something names it (an offer, a notice, a message), and when a gathering it means to go to starts or ends.

**Everything happens the way a person does it.** They apply, sign, move in, ask their bank to pay, write, turn up. Nothing happens to them automatically, and they know only what they've seen or been told.

**Households** ([households.md](households.md)): a citizen lives in a household. The rules on homes and leaving town are the **leaseholder's**, for the household: the budget is a third of household income, the search is for the bedrooms the occupancy standard asks for, and the whole household moves and leaves together. Other adults follow the leaseholder and pay a share of the rent. Children act on their own only from 18, when they open a bank account. Voice (petitions) and complaints are the leaseholders'.

## Who a citizen is, and how it decides (the mind)

| | What | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Values** | Schwartz's ten, drawn once per person from the world's profile (each value's mean and spread): what a person believes in. | Schwartz (1992); the profile's figures, Schwartz and Bardi's pan-cultural order (a sourced placeholder) |
| **Temperament** | From values:<br>• openness (stimulation, self-direction): exploring;<br>• conformity (conformity, tradition): copying what most do;<br>• prestige (achievement, power): copying the respected. | Dwarf Fortress's facets beside values; Boyd and Richerson's conformist bias; Rogers |
| **Needs** | Five, each weighed 1 to 2 as values say:<br>• subsistence (hedonism): goods held;<br>• shelter (security, tradition): a home;<br>• security (security): savings against outgoings;<br>• social (benevolence, conformity): living with others, and up to three people talked with in the past month;<br>• status (power, achievement): work. | Humanoid Agents; D2A; Dwarf Fortress (values set needs). The mapping is ours, to test against data |
| **How to decide** | CONSUMAT's four ways, by satisfaction (needs met, as the person weighs them; aspiration 60%) and certainty (the share of the options they know; 50%, less by fear where money's at stake):<br>• satisfied and sure: **repeat**;<br>• satisfied, unsure: **imitate** (what people they know do, weighed more by the conformist);<br>• unsatisfied, sure: **compare** (the best of the options they know);<br>• unsatisfied, unsure: **deliberate**. Deliberating appraises each option's facts (an offer's typed terms: money, a home and its bedrooms, hours, goods, company, time) against needs, adds what it means to them (belief), and chooses by the logit of that plus what's learned, the more open exploring more. | Jager (2000); Jager and Janssen (2012); utility AI's considerations (Dave Mark) |
| **Learning** | Experience-weighted attraction per area (shop, job, home, practice, membership): each outcome's payoff, and others' payoffs as they tell them, weighted below one's own (δ). An untried option has the area's prior. | Camerer and Ho (1999); hearing others' payoffs between choices is ours |
| **Knowledge** | Only what it perceives and is told: the listings and postings it looks at, the buildings around it, the people it shares a home, workplace or gathering with, and what they say when it catches up with them. | Talk of the Town (Ryan et al. 2017); Granovetter (1973) |
| **Feelings** | Emotions by appraisal, each about someone or something, each fading by its half-life (joy 7 days, distress and hope 14, fear 30, anger 60, gratitude 90):<br>• **anger** with a landlord who raises the rent (by the share of income it takes) or gives notice to go, with an employer who stops paying, with the town when waste isn't collected;<br>• **fear** for money when savings run short (it makes a person less sure where money's at stake, so they weigh their options afresh);<br>• **gratitude** to someone whose tip found them a home or work, and to the union when a raise it won comes through. | OCC (Ortony, Clore and Collins 1988), as FAtiMA computes it |
| **Ties** | Affinity with each person known. It grows by catching up, by gatherings together and by gratitude, and fades without contact (half-life 180 days). A citizen catches up more often with those it's closer to, and keeps as contacts (`max_contacts`) those it's close to or has seen in the past month. | Comme il Faut; the Ensemble engine (McCoy et al. 2014; Samuel et al. 2015) |
| **Belief** | The world's teachings (statements of kind `teaching`: what a group or the union asks people to hold), each held with a strength, starting from what the world gives the person (`beliefs`: members strongly, others anywhere from barely to a good deal). It changes three ways:<br>• it fades monthly;<br>• it strengthens at the teacher's gatherings with how many others came, keeping to it at a cost of their time (the way people come to a belief they don't hold);<br>• it strengthens when someone says they hold it, if the citizen holds it at all (more for the conformist): words alone don't convert.<br>Asked how things are, a citizen says it holds a teaching once it's firm (`tell_at_bps`), or that it leans towards it once it's its own at all (`affiliate_bps`, as a census asks); only the firm statement moves others.<br>It adds to what the teacher's gatherings are worth, and holding what the union teaches makes joining it likelier. | Henrich (2009), credibility-enhancing displays; Boyd and Richerson (1985); Stark and Lofland (1965), conversion through networks and participation (to confirm). Calibrated: fading against the census's fall in affiliation |
| **Goals** | A need unmet for `unmet_days` (money: security below `security_bps`; company: social below `social_bps`) becomes a goal, pursued by one method after another, each given `method_days`, in the order the person's values favour. It's met when the need recovers, and given up when nothing's left, until `rest_days` later.<br>• **Making ends meet:** better-paid work, looked for every month (achievement, stimulation); spending less, saving `spend_less_saving_bps` of income (security, conformity); a cheaper home at the lease's end, under `cheaper_bps` of the old rent for the first month of the hunt (security over tradition).<br>• **More company:** catching up twice as often (benevolence); giving gatherings real thought (stimulation, benevolence). | Hierarchical task networks, one level (Erol, Hendler and Nau 1994); CONSUMAT's dissatisfaction as what starts one |
| **Asking the model** | The sparse mind, **off unless a run turns it on** (`--consult`). Deliberating about joining the union at a new job, or starting a business with premises in reach, a citizen asks once how much each option would matter to someone like it. It decides at the next wake, its own draw from the answer's spread shifting the cited rate (`sway.consult`). Answers are remembered for each kind of person and thing, and logged. | docs/design/ai-citizens.md section 4 (after Affordable Generative Agents, AgentTorch, GenWorld) |
| **Recorded** | Every decision the mind makes (area, way, choice, each option's score) goes with the citizen's next action. Full logs keep it as a `decision` annotation, exported as `citizen_decisions.jsonl`. | docs/design/ai-citizens.md section 7 |

## What a citizen does

| | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Rent budget** | With income: rent ≤ 33% of household income. Without: only if savings cover 6 months' rent. | Bank of England housing model (Baptista et al. 2016, §3.4.1): renters "bid a fixed fraction of its income (set at 33%)" |
| **Choosing a home** | Among the listings seen that are affordable and have the bedrooms the household needs, the mind's choice: each offer's rent and the home's bedrooms appraised (room beyond the bedrooms needed counts for a little); homes friends named count as others having gone there. One application a week, never the same listing twice. Still hunting after `accept_fewer_bedrooms_after_days`, one bedroom fewer will do, and so on. | Replaces the BoE's "highest quality they can afford" (rent standing for quality). One year: median rent share 23.3 to 20.5% (target 20.2%) |
| **Signing and moving in** | Sign the landlord's offer and set up a monthly standing order for rent, paid in advance. Then move in as a separate physical act, trying again the next day until the home is free (by time, not by how often they happen to look: 2026-09-30). | Rent in advance is standard practice. Signing and moving in are separate: someone can hold a lease before the last tenant has left |
| **Staying** | Never ends a tenancy early. Leaves when the term ends or the landlord's notice runs out (making ends meet, it then looks for somewhere cheaper first). | BoE model §3.4.2 |
| **A rent increase** | Given notice of one, the leaseholder is angry with the landlord, the more so the more of their income it takes. It keeps the home and pays the new rent from when it starts while it can bear it: up to half of household income (`rent_bearable_share_bps`), in rental stress past a third. Beyond that, it gives notice and looks for somewhere else. The new rent is paid on the old one's days, from the first after it starts; the old standing order stops after its last payment. (Until 2026-09-30 it gave notice past a third: at the first annual review about a third of renting households did, and most of them left town.) | Many renters pay over 30% of income and stay: rental stress, as income and housing surveys measure it (ABS, *Survey of Income and Housing*). The half is a placeholder |
| **Rent the bank couldn't pay** | Told by the bank that a rent payment (or its share of the rent) failed, it still owes it: it pays it as soon as its balance covers it, before shopping, oldest first. | Rent missed is owed; a standing order that fails isn't retried by the bank |
| **Work** | Unemployed, in the labour force and below retirement age: apply for a posting seen, one a week. Which employer is the mind's: each offer's wage and hours appraised, with what's learned of each employer (one that paid as agreed, or didn't, is remembered; what friends say of theirs counts); employers friends named or work for count as others having gone there. | Search on the postings; the labour-market literature (Dosi et al.) is the planned refinement |
| **A better job** | In work: looking at the postings every month while unsatisfied (CONSUMAT) or making ends meet (a goal), and otherwise now and then, the more open the more often. It applies only to better-paid postings elsewhere. Offered one: take it, give notice at the old job (the contract's notice), and start once it's worked out. | Replaces Lengnick's (2013) flat monthly chance (π = 0.1) |
| **Unpaid wages** | Wages that haven't come for a pay period plus five days: resign (ending it now), record the wages owed as a debt against the employer (a claim), and be angry with them. | Employees are creditors for unpaid wages |
| **Retiring and support** | At the world's retirement age (none unless the world sets one), give notice at work and claim the support the world names for retirees. Out of work below it (and in the labour force), claim support for the unemployed. A claim stops when it no longer fits; a refused one isn't made again until a year older. Support in retirement counts as income that keeps a household in town; support while out of work doesn't, and isn't counted once in work. | The world's support payments, from its profile (docs/behaviour/boundary.md) |
| **In the labour force** | Some working-age adults aren't: they don't look for work or claim unemployment support. Drawn for everyone at the start (children included, for when they're adults), for newcomers by the migration office, and for children born here at birth (`participation_bps`). | The world's profile |
| **Spending** | Each shopping trip spends for the time since the last (up to two fortnights), since a trip is made when the citizen is awake, often days after it was due. A month's spending: out of income, what isn't saved (the saving ratio; more while making ends meet) less housing, never below essentials; plus a little out of savings above a buffer of 3 months' income (a wealth effect). Income is a month's worth of what came in over the last 90 days, less refunds. | The household saving ratio; the literature on consumption and wealth; the world's figures from its profile |
| **Shopping** | Every fortnight, half a month's spending buys goods at a shop, at its listed price, ordered and paid with the order. Which shop is the mind's: each shop's offer appraised for what the budget buys there; the usual shop kept while it serves well and the household is sure of its options; shops friends use count as others going. Each delivery teaches how fully that shop serves. A shortfall is tried at another known shop, chosen by what's learned, up to 5 shops a trip. | Lengnick (2013): a fortnight's worth, β = 5; the mind replaces his ψ and ξ rules. CONSUMAT; EWA |
| **Using things up** | Weekly, it uses up (the task `consume`) what it holds, spread over the weeks until the next trip, at home (bringing home what's elsewhere), or at the shop that keeps it without a home. | Lengnick's households consume what they buy; spreading it to the next trip is a placeholder |
| **A union** | Starting a job, it may join the union (apply, sign the membership offered, pay dues monthly). The chance is the profile's rate for that kind of work, shifted by:<br>• its solidarity (benevolence, universalism);<br>• what membership has done for it (a raise won, pay lost on strike: learned);<br>• the share of co-workers it knows who belong (weighed by conformity);<br>• whether it holds with what the union teaches;<br>• what the sparse mind says, when a run has it.<br>A member tells the union when it starts work elsewhere or is out of work (a `membership_update`), votes only in a protected action ballot about its own workplace (turnout and yes by solidarity; security against), stops work when the union calls a stoppage at its employer and goes back when it calls the return, and expects the raise an enterprise agreement gives. | ABS, *Trade union membership*, August 2024; docs/behaviour/unions.md |
| **Starting a business** | Monthly, working-age and in the labour force, with premises for sale of a kind the world has a venture for, and savings for their price, the venture's capital and three months' housing and essentials: the month's chance (`found_bps`), swayed by openness and achievement and by the sparse mind when a run has it. It founds the business (holding its first position, where its rules run) and pays in the capital. | docs/behaviour/entry.md; the chance is a placeholder, to calibrate against business entry rates |
| **Catching up** | Every `catch_up_days` (twice as often while seeking company), it asks someone it knows how things are, more often those it's closer to. Asked, it answers with what it does, in its own phrasing:<br>• where it shops and how that shop serves it;<br>• whom it works for and whether they pay;<br>• whether it's in the union;<br>• the gatherings it goes to;<br>• the teachings it holds firmly.<br>What it's told counts, for `news_days`, as what that person does (imitation), and as their payoffs (learning). | Word of mouth (below); conformist transmission (Boyd and Richerson 1985) |
| **Taking part** | It sees the world's gatherings (statements carrying a practice), and may come where a role is open to it (anyone, or members of the host). A week ahead the mind decides: the company it found there, or as many as it knows go, and what the teaching means to it, against the time it takes. Its routine at first is what its memberships say (a membership whose terms say it attends). Going is being in the gathering's space for its time. After, it learns how it was, by how many came (turned away from a full hall, it counts for nothing), and those it met become people it knows. | Versu's social practices (Evans and Short 2014). Target: 50.0% of adults take part in a social group over a year (ABS, General Social Survey 2019) |
| **Complaining** | Waste going uncollected at home makes the leaseholder angry with whoever should collect it (the town): half-way at `complain_after_days`' worth, fading by anger's half-life. Once it passes their own threshold (the conformist bears it longer: about 8 to 20 days' worth), while a week's worth is still there, they write to the town (a `complaint`) with the week's chance (`complain_bps`), once until it's collected. | The service-request literature (e.g. Stowers 2022); docs/behaviour/services.md |
| **Voice: starting a petition** | A tenant is *aggrieved* while angry with their landlord (at or above `aggrieved_bps`: a notice to go lasts about 95 days, a rent rise the longer the more it takes), or while rent is ≥ 40% of income. If no tenant-protection petition is open, an aggrieved tenant starts one with probability 3% per wake. It demands a law (90 days' notice; rent rises capped at 5% a year), with its Institutional Grammar breakdown, tagged `tenant_protection`, and closed after 180 days. | Hirschman (1970): voice |
| **Voice: who signs at all** | Each person signs petitions at all with probability 44%, drawn once; only they sign or start petitions. | Australian Election Study 2016: 44% had signed a written petition in five years (to confirm against the tables) |
| **Voice: signing** | A tenant signs when aggrieved, or once the share of the town that has signed (the organiser's latest published count, at most every 3 days) reaches their own threshold. Thresholds are roughly normal (mean 25%, sd 10%), half the person's conformity and half their own draw: the conformist waits for others. | Granovetter (1978) |
| **Elections: the roll** | Of voting age (or new to town) and not enrolled: enrols with the town, once, as the law requires (`electoral_town`). | Compulsory enrolment (Victoria) |
| **Elections: approval** | Weekly, its approval of the town's government (−10,000 to 10,000) moves toward its own fortunes (satisfaction) less its anger at the town (a waste complaint's, say), remembered over about half a year (`approval_memory_weeks` 26). A reader of the paper (`reads_news_bps`, drawn once) moves it by each item about the government's tone (`news_weight_bps`). | Retrospective voting (Fiorina 1981); economic voting (Lewis-Beck and Stegmaier 2000). The weights are placeholders |
| **Elections: standing** | Given notice of an election, while nominations are open, it stands with a small chance (`stand_bps`), more with drive (achievement, power) and the worse it thinks the town is run. Its platform is its two strongest values. | Placeholder, to calibrate against candidates per vacancy |
| **Elections: voting** | On the roll, with the ballot open: it votes with the chance `turnout_bps` (voting is compulsory; some don't), once, in secret (visible only to the town). It ranks every candidate: itself first if it stands; the incumbent by its approval; a challenger by how well their platform's values are its own (`platform_weight_bps`). | Preferential voting; retrospective and values-based choice (placeholders) |
| **School** | A child of school age (the world's `school.ages`) not at school asks the school for a place (once a month) and goes when offered. At the end of school they leave. | docs/behaviour/education.md |
| **Too ill to work** | With a condition of a band nobody works with (the world's `care.off_work`: hospital-level), out of the workplace until better, then back. | Placeholder (docs/behaviour/health.md) |
| **Care** | (A child too: their parents would take them.) With a condition of a kind the clinic sees, not yet treated: to the clinic with the chance `care.seek_bps`, waiting there and asking to be seen; home once seen (felt), better, or after `wait_days`. | Placeholder (docs/behaviour/health.md) |
| **Floods** | Flooded out of home (or finding it under water): to the relief centre (`relief`), home again tried a day on. | docs/behaviour/floods.md |
| **Taking** | In need and at work where the employer keeps goods: rarely, some taken and brought home (`crime`). A tribunal's order to pay: paid when we can. | docs/behaviour/crime.md |
| **The daily round** | At each wake: where we belong now (work in working hours, school in school hours, the clinic while waiting, a gathering while it lasts, else home), and there if we're not; awake again when that changes. Such wakes only move us; the full plan is weekly, on news, and at a gathering. Off sick: a notice to our employer, and we don't go in. A move the world refuses isn't tried again before things change (a home pulled down: never). | docs/behaviour/daily-round.md |
| **A home of our own** | A lot whose title is ours, with a dwelling we live in on it: no rent and no hunting for a rental; the mortgage repayment counts as housing; rates (and penalty interest, fines) paid when they come, keeping a month's essentials; no tenant's grievances; if we'd leave town, we sell first (at its valuation, cutting the price monthly), pay off the mortgage, and go. With our mortgage in default, we sell and rent (0.33.0). | docs/behaviour/ownership.md |
| **Buying a home** | Renting, with income and the deposit and costs saved, now and then: the cheapest home for sale with the bedrooms we need, on a mortgage; once it's ours, notice on the lease, and in. Inherited houses we don't live in: sold. | docs/behaviour/ownership.md |
| **Utilities** | Holding a home and living there: signs up with the energy retailer. Bills addressed to us paid when they come, if we can, before anything else; what they come to a month counts with the rent in outgoings. | docs/behaviour/utilities.md |
| **The tribunal** | A notice of rent increase that breaks the law (as we read it): to the tribunal with the chance `tribunal.challenge_bps`, paying the old rent meanwhile, and the new one only if it's found valid. A debt owed us still unpaid after `apply_after_days`: an application, with the chance `apply_bps`; an order to pay that's unpaid a day past its date, a request to enforce it. | docs/behaviour/justice.md (placeholders) |
| **Leaving town** | After 90 days without a home, or 180 days with nobody in the household earning and less money than a month's rent or essentials: give notice, take all savings out of town, and leave with the household. The thresholds stretch with tradition and security, from half to one and a half times. | Hirschman (1970): exit. Thresholds are placeholders to calibrate |

The rates the person's values shift (`sway`) move symmetrically about the town's mean, so the town's rates stay as calibrated while people differ.

## Attention: what a citizen looks at (D43)

A citizen doesn't receive the housing or job market. It looks when it needs to, the way a person reads the listings when hunting:
- **Needing a home:** standing rental listings within its budget and with the bedrooms needed, dearest first, the top 20.
- **Needing work, or looking for better:** standing job postings, best-paid first, the top 20.
- **Every wake:** a glance at the petitions board. Signature counts arrive as an ordinary report.
- **Always:** its own records, money and holdings, the laws, enterprise agreements, the town's gatherings and teachings, and prices.

## Position: what a citizen can see around it (D44)

A citizen sees the lots touching where it lives, works and gathers ("Around you"): what's on each, and how full it is. In its own buildings it sees who's there. The view moves when it moves. It also tells whether it's actually in its workplace before leaving it.

## Word of mouth (D44, D45)

Word of mouth is people telling people, over ordinary messages; the platform passes nothing along.

| | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Ties** | The people a citizen shares, or has shared, a home, workplace or gathering with, plus those it talks with. It keeps `max_contacts`: those it's closest to, or has seen in the past month. | Granovetter, "The Strength of Weak Ties" (1973); Dunbar's ~15 "sympathy group" for the cap (a placeholder) |
| **Asking around** | Starting a hunt for a home, a citizen asks all its contacts with probability `ask_home_bps`; for work, `ask_work_bps`. Still hunting after `ask_again_days`, it asks again. | Homes: 37% of renters ask friends or family (Apartments.com/NAA 2026). Work: 69% of young job seekers used friends and relatives over a year (Holzer 1987, via Ioannides and Loury 2004) |
| **Passing on** | Asked, a citizen names a building where it can see room: for a home, its own building or one next to it; for work, only its own workplace. It says only what it can see (`pass_on_bps`). | Placeholder |
| **Following up** | Told about a building, a citizen searches the listings or postings there first. A home or job found there makes it grateful to whoever said so. | Schmidt, *ASR* 2025 (qualitative) |

Code citizens understand only their own phrasing (`ASKING`, `CATCHING_UP` and the household phrases in the module). A language-model citizen understands anything, and a code citizen won't understand its free words: a limit of code citizens, not of the world. A code citizen acts on the building named, not the words, so there's no coded distortion; that arrives with language-model citizens.

## Calibration (docs/calibration.md)

Outcomes to measure, never set. The town's figures are in [docs/calibration.md](../calibration.md), and each step's before and after in docs/design/ai-citizens.md "Progress". The latest year, in the 1,000-person town over two seeds:
- employment to population 66–67% (target 63.9%);
- median rent share about 20.5% (target 20.2%);
- group participation 52% (target 50.0%);
- hires after a tip 16–50 a year (target about half of hires: still far below).

Saving is off: it's negative in the first year, and 22–25% over five (target 6.5%).

## Leaves out (for now)

- Voice beyond petitions, complaints and elections: protest, contacting councillors, campaigning for a candidate.
- Knowing which laws already exist (a citizen may petition for a law already passed).
- Buying homes; commuting; housing quality beyond price and bedrooms; renegotiating rent.
- Moving house while housed: looking for somewhere cheaper or better while keeping the home, and giving notice once a new lease is signed. A household hunts only once it's without a home, so a rent it can't bear means notice first, then the hunt (the realistic order is the other way round).
- The other social moves: help, snub, gossip, reputation.
