# The town generator (`worlds/generator/generate.ts`, v0.49.0)

Turns a short spec (`worlds/town.yaml`) and a **profile** (`worlds/profiles/`, docs/profiles.md: every place-specific figure, with its source) into a full, explicit contracts v0.5 world config. The generator's code holds no place's figures; what it needs beyond the profile it derives by general formulas:
- **a factory's item:** its labour share of the world price, as hours at the factory wage; its input share, as kg of inputs;
- **a shop's item:** its retail price from the purchases share; its serving time from the labour share, at the shop wage;
- **shops' opening staff:** from the sales the town's expected incomes would make, by the citizens' own rule of spending: wages after what employers withhold, pensions by how often they are paid, the payment to the unemployed where there is one, the rent of the homes that are let, never less than the essentials, and the profile's share a year of savings beyond the months kept in hand;
- **an hour's pay and the working day:** a pay period's wage over the hours worked in it; the working day is the profile's working week over the days people work;
- **an item of goods:** the town's unit; its price is the profile's `prices.goods_world` if stated (checked for being a size whole numbers can carry), else what half an hour of factory work makes;
- **a firm's opening money:** its working capital (a shop: a month's stock and three months' wages; a factory: a month's wages and a month's inputs), never a stated sum (docs/profiles.md);
- **world prices** move as the profile's `world_price_process` says; a spec's `shocks` script changes to a price or to demand (docs/behaviour/boundary.md);
- **the world market's demand**, if the spec doesn't set it: what the factories' opening staff make, less the town's own purchases (a boundary condition, set by rule). It's deterministic from the spec's seed, using the kernel's keyed randomness. The kernel never generates anything; it receives this output. Everything generated is example **content** (D23).

**What it makes:**
- **Land:** a grid of cells, each a lot on the town's registry (a `lot` record naming its cell). The southern row is the river.
- **Catalogue:** the kinds of building, each with its materials, hours of building and demolition work, and least time to build ([building.md](building.md)), including a `park` and the builder's `yard`. Three resource kinds: `goods` (items), `inputs` (kg) and `materials` (kg). Three tasks: `make` (a factory's), `serve` (a shop's) and `consume` (a household's). A working day of 8 hours.
- **Buildings:** apartment blocks (12 units: 3 one-bedroom, 7 two, 2 three) and houses (three or four bedrooms) owned by landlords; shops and factories owning their premises; the builder's yard (a workplace for its crew of up to 12; it starts with 4); a town hall. Every other lot is recorded as the town's. A home's physical capacity is 2 per bedroom plus 2, or what the profile states (`homes`).
- **Entities:** the outside world, the town, one bank, a builder, landlords, shops, factories, and the people (and a retirement fund, in a town that asks for one: `retirement_fund: true`), in **households** drawn from the profile's census shares (extended households among them where it states any), with ages and birth dates ([households.md](households.md)).
- **Money:** every account is held at the bank, except the outside world's, which it holds itself.
- **Holdings:** the builder starts with a house's worth of materials (120 t) in its yard.
- **Genesis records:**
  - the example legal code (`worlds/law/basic.json`): charter, property, tenancy, planning and tax law;
  - ownership claims;
  - each household (a `household` record);
  - leases and jobs already in force (tenancies part-way through, 12–24-month terms; a lease is the leaseholder's, naming the household and the account the rent is paid from);
  - the standing orders that pay rent, the other adults' shares of the rent, and wages;
  - the town's `signatories` list for its account (the finance officer's position) and its payments procedure;
  - the budget the town opens with (`budget-year-1`, adopted by its council before the year began, with the proposal, ballot, votes and result it rests on; its lines are the ones the mayor's office tables each year: [government.md](government.md), "The budget year"). `opening_budget: false` in a spec gives a town whose council has adopted none;
  - who owns each firm (its `shareholders` register): its owners, the outside world, or (a factory) the outside world and the households that hold shares directly; with a retirement fund, the fund too, and its register of members ([firm-ownership.md](firm-ownership.md)).
- **Positions and participants:** every organisation acts through positions, each held at the start by a system participant running the job's module:
  - the mayor: with `mayor: llm`, a language-model participant (weekly cadence; the laws and the forms on file as standing documents; until a real model is used, the mock, with `city.mayor_code` as its brain, see docs/design/m2-llm-participants.md), or with `mayor: code` the rule-following code mayor (the all-code control);
  - the finance officer's budget brief (`budget_lines`): one line, parks, at the builder's price for a park (as the builder publishes it);
  - the city's finance officer, planning officer, inspector, rates officer, council clerk, and five councillors at positions −80, −40, 0, 40 and 80 ([government.md](government.md));
  - the bank's payment system; the world market (`world.trade`: materials at $1.25 a kg and inputs at $2.50 a kg, and goods (imports) at $66; buying the factories' goods at $60, `exports_per_month` split by the size of their works);
  - the builder's site foreman (what each kind of building takes, from the catalogue, as its own business knowledge; it publishes its prices, and the mayor, finance and landlords read them there, D57), landlords' letting offices, the managers of shops (`firm.retailer`: serving takes 1.5 hours an item; it opens at 1.1 × marginal cost; it starts with 3 staff, room for 6, 100 items in stock, and its own shareholders register) and factories (`firm.producer`: the task `make`, 10 kg of inputs and 1.5 hours an item; wholesale at $60; each factory's `shareholders` register, every household's leaseholder in proportion to the household's savings; opening stock of a month's inputs and a week's goods);
  - a union ([unions.md](unions.md)): its organiser (bargaining with the factories, shops and builder), its account, and its members at the start (each employee with the profile's membership rate for their kind of work; the membership agreement, dues by standing order, and the union's register); citizens join on starting a job at the same rates, and see enterprise agreements;
  - services ([services.md](services.md)): the variables `waste` (homes) and `wear` (parks, the hall), the tasks `collect` and `maintain`, and the resource `waste`, from the profile's figures; the town's works crew in work from the start at the hall (the profile's staff per 1,000 residents, filled first); the works office and the outside world's landfill; the budget's staff and services lines, and the proposal form's fields for them; citizens complain to the town;
  - public finance ([public-finance.md](public-finance.md)): the outside world's tax law on pay (the profile's charges) and wage orders written net of it; its support law (the profile's payments); the outside world's valuer (land per lot: the profile's median house price less the builder's price for the commonest house) and grants office (the profile's grant per resident); the town's rating law (`law-rates`, its rate in the dollar set so the town raises the profile's rates per resident) and rates officer;
  - every person, as themselves (`<person>-self`), with their sex as a trait. Their controller's params carry the world's **ventures**: one, a shop, a template for the new organisation's first position (its channels, and `firm.retailer` with this town's shop settings, the premises and founder filled in when someone founds one; capital beyond the premises $50,000, a placeholder), and `found_bps` 200 (a month's chance, a placeholder) ([entry.md](entry.md)).

**Starting conditions (0.31.0).** A description may name scenarios (`scenario: wet-year+lean-council`): named shifts to the description and to the profile's figures, applied before anything is generated, and for the weather a choice among the climate's own years. With none (or `baseline`) the town is exactly as before. docs/scenarios.md.

## Homes by income, and payments in force (generator 0.49.0, 2026-10-12)

**What was wrong.** Homes were dealt by size alone: households sorted by the bedrooms they need, each given the smallest home that met the standard, its rent whatever that home's bedrooms cost. Income was never looked at, and what income there was counted wages only: a pensioner had nothing coming in until the claim made on the first morning was decided and a whole period had passed (14 to 61 days). In the 1,000-person town 73 of 239 leases (Victoria's profile) opened with no wage earner; in the 3,000-person town of the living pilots about 28% of let households opened in a home nobody in them could pay for, and most of a first year's arrears, notices and evictions were those households (docs/behaviour/justice.md, "Where arrears come from"). docs/decisions.md, 2026-10-12, "Homes by income".

**What a household has coming in** is what its people count themselves (citizen.default's own reckoning): pay as agreed, what a business of their own is expected to bring, and payments in force. Where the profile's rent shares are of net income (`of: "net"`), pay is counted after what the employer withholds.

**Payments in force** (`support.take_up_bps`, by payment). Someone the support law already entitles as the year begins has the payment, as often as the profile's take-up for it says: a pension by its age; a flat payment for someone out of work and looking (the first the law lists that asks no record of contributions). The claim and the grant are papers on file from before the year (`benefit_claim`, `benefit_decision`, each `brought_forward`), the grant naming the day the next payment falls due, spread evenly over the payment's period. The office pays from its file when it first opens it (`world.transfers` 0.6.0); the claimant draws it from the first day and does not claim again (citizen 0.52.0). Drawn on a stream of its own (`payments-in-force`).
- **Not opened this way:** a payment reckoned from a record of contributions (nobody has a record on the first day: the insurance payments of three profiles), a payment to a household, one that turns on children. Their people claim in the year, as before.
- **Those not drawing a payment at the start claim it when they first look, and are granted**: nothing in the citizen keeps anyone from a payment the law gives. So take-up below 100% is a delay of the office's processing time and one period, not a standing share of non-claimants. Real non-receipt is mostly a means test the profiles' laws do not state.
- A payment not listed in `take_up_bps` opens with nobody drawing it.

**Taken in.** A household with nothing coming in and not six months of the cheapest rent put by does not rent alone: it lives with a household that can carry it, as kin (docs/behaviour/households.md, "Taken in"), as far as the profile's share of households that hold kin goes. What is left over is without a home of its own.

**Who owns.** The profile's share of the houses are their households' own, as before. Whose: where the profile states ownership by the age of the household's head (`ownership.owner_bps_by_age`), a household's chance is in proportion to its age's share (drawn without replacement on the `ownership` stream); where it does not, the households that need the most room, as before. Among the owners, the neediest have the most bedrooms.

**Who rents what** (`tenancy.rent_share_of_income`: the share of the place's renting households in each band of rent over income).
- Each renting household draws its share from the bands (evenly within a band; the open band from its lower bound to twice it, at most everything) on a stream of its own (`rent-share`). Share times what it had coming in is **the rent it carries**. With nothing coming in: a sixth of what its leaseholder has put by (the citizen's own rule: six months of rent in hand).
- **What it had coming in when it took the home**: someone now out of work and looking was in work then, and is counted at the town's usual pay (the mean over its jobs); their payment while they look is a stopgap, and no home was taken on it. This is where a town's opening hardship comes from now: households whose income has fallen since they took their home, as many as the spec's share out of work makes.
- Households, by the rent they carry, and let homes, by rent, are matched in order (dearer homes to those who carry more) so that the fewest households are left without a home and, among such matches, the rents are as near what each household carries as the town's homes allow (the distance as a share of the rent; an exact least-cost match that keeps the order).
- **Two limits:** a home must hold the household's bodies; and nobody holds a home whose rent is more than everything they had coming in when they took it, unless their savings cover six months of it.
- **The room its standard asks for bounds what a household carries, both ways** (the citizen's own way of choosing a home: the bedrooms it needs, and among those the rent). Below: a household whose drawn share would not pay for those bedrooms carries the cheapest home that has them, if that is within half of what it has coming in (what the citizen bears before giving a home up). Above: nobody carries more than the dearest home of that size. Among households that carry the same, those whose share drew more come first. So homes go by size as far as income bears, and within a size the dearer to those who carry more; a household that cannot bear its room is in a smaller home. (A first trial matched on the drawn rent alone: the best-paid couples took the largest homes, and by the year's end a tenth of the Victorian town's households were overcrowded, 38 where there had been 2. `runs/homes-by-income-2026-10-12/trial1`.)

**What it cannot do.** A town has five rents (by bedrooms) and is nearly full, so who pays what share is mostly decided by its incomes against those five rents: the distribution the town opens with is reported beside the profile's and is **not fitted to it** (the table in docs/decisions.md). Rents are not redrawn from incomes: they are the profile's rents for real homes, and a landlord's review would pull each back to its building's.

**The town says what it opened with** in its description (`meta.description`): payments in force; households taken in by kin; households renting on their savings; households without a home.

**A profile that states no `tenancy.rent_share_of_income`** is dealt its homes by size, with owners by the house, as before; one that states no `support.take_up_bps` opens with nothing in payment (tested: the old counts to the lease).

## An ordinary day's caseload (`opening_caseload`, generator 0.48.0, 2026-10-12)

**Why.** A town generated with every rent book at nought has nothing before its tribunal for months: rent must be owed for the landlord's patience, then the law's notice, then the tribunal's time to a hearing, before anything can be decided (docs/behaviour/justice.md, "The profiles' figures": no profile gives a first order before about day 113, most about day 140). Two living pilots of 120 days could show no possession order, whoever sat. A real town has tenancies at every stage on any day; a person who takes an office over finds its files part-way through.

**What it is.** Off unless the description says `opening_caseload: true`; it needs the profile's law on possession and a tribunal. It is world content, drawn on the generator's own stream (`opening-caseload`) from the seed: the same description and seed give the same town, to the word, and a town opened this way is a twin of itself as any other is. A town without it is exactly the town it was.

- **Who.** The let households the town already holds that cannot pay their rent: nothing coming in that covers it (pay and payments in force), and less than a month's rent put by. These are the households the town's own mechanism carries into arrears in its first month (docs/behaviour/justice.md, "Where arrears come from"). **Nobody is added, and no rate of arrears is assumed**: no profile carries one. The option does not make more tenancies fall behind; it stops them all falling behind on the same day.
- **Where each stands.** As if its first rent had been missed on a day spread evenly over the length of the procedure (a landlord's patience of 60 days; the law's notice and a day; the tribunal's time to a hearing; the days from an order to its date). The stage is what the law's own days put such a tenancy at. Past the notice's date only tenants who stayed are still in the home, so those are kept as often as the profile's `stay_past_notice_bps` says (a placeholder), and the rest are placed again among the stages before it. So days left are spread: notices' dates over the notice period, days to answer over the tribunal's time, orders' dates over the order's days. Nothing falls due all in one week.
- **As papers**, dated as the year begins and saying in their words how long before it each was made (`data.brought_forward`):
  - every such tenancy: the landlord's **statement of the rent account** to the tenant (`rent_statement`: what was owed as the year began, how many rents, the oldest how many days before);
  - past the landlord's patience: its **notice to vacate**, with its date (ahead, or already past);
  - past the notice's date: the landlord's **application for possession**, and the tribunal's **notice of hearing** to both sides with the day the tenant may answer by;
  - past that day: the tribunal's **order for possession**, with its date ahead.
- **Who reads them.** Nothing is done by a paper; each is read by whoever would read it:
  - the landlord's rent book counts what the statement says was owed with what falls due in the year, and owed since then; a landlord run by code, opening its files, takes each tenancy up at the stage its papers put it at (as its clerk's books do for a seat);
  - the tenant owes it as it owes any rent the bank could not pay, a rent at a time, and pays when it can; a notice to vacate is read as any is (a tenant still in the home after a notice whose date had passed stayed: that is not drawn again); a notice of hearing is answered; an order is obeyed or not, by the tenant's own draw;
  - the tribunal takes the cases its own file says are listed, with the days their notices gave, and lists none of them again; its member's books show them.

**After homes by income (0.49.0), the same town** (3,000 people, `ke-nakuru-2026`, seed 402): 466 let homes (the dear houses stand empty: no household's income carries them); **14 tenancies open with rent owed** (households where someone is out of work as the year begins and nothing is put by), 10 of them under a notice to vacate (5 with its date ahead), 5 before the tribunal (2 with the time to answer still running: days 9 and 34), 3 with an order made (days 9, 9, 16); 9 of the 45 landlords with tenants have one (the pilots' landlord-38: 12 tenancies, one with rent owed and under notice, none before the tribunal; before, 14 tenancies, 6 with rent owed, 4 under notice, 3 before the tribunal). The tribunal's member finds 5 cases on the list on the first day where there were 49. Also on the first day: 24 pensions in payment, 128 households living with kin, 19 renting on savings, 60 households without a home. The count follows the town's own mechanism, as it did: nothing was set.

**Before that, in the fourth living pilot's town** (generator 0.48.0; 714 let homes): 198 tenancies open with rent owed; of those 93 are under a notice to vacate (44 with its date still ahead, spread over days 1 to 31); 49 have an application before the tribunal, 32 of them with the tenant's time to answer still running (days to answer by: 2, 2, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 8, 9, 10, 12, 12, 16, 19 … 42) and 17 with an order made and its date ahead (days 3 to 30). The 198 was the generator's doing: about 28% of let households opened in a home nobody in them earned enough to pay for (the opening-day artefact, fixed in 0.49.0, above).

**Leaves out** (listed in the decisions log): a warrant already out on the first day; a plan to pay already ordered; the opening caseloads of every other office (support claims part-decided, complaints open, permits before planning, loans behind, a council's ballots open, unpaid invoices); a household's savings and record consistent with months of arrears (the tenant's record meets the notice on day 1, not before the year); landlords whose patience differs from the default; the arrears list of a desk that hears arrears.
