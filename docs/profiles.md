# Profiles: where a place's figures live

Oikos is a general platform. **Its code is the same for every place and time.** Everything true of one place (its census shares, life tables, wages, rents, support payments, industry shares, currency) lives in a **profile**, with its source, and reaches a world through its generator.

## The layers

| Layer | Holds | Place-specific? |
|---|---|---|
| Kernel and contracts | Physics: money, goods, work, records, time | Never |
| Behaviour modules (`participants/src/behaviour/`) | General rules ("a person retires at an age and claims support"; "a shop restocks by Lengnick's band") with neutral names. Defaults are placeholders or "none" (e.g. no retirement age unless the world sets one) | Never |
| Generators (`worlds/generator/`) | A general procedure for making a kind of world (a town) from a spec and a profile, deriving what it needs by general formulas (hours per item from an industry's labour share and a wage) | Never |
| **Profiles** (`worlds/profiles/<id>.json`) | Every place-specific figure, each with its source, and the calibration targets for that place | **Yes: this is where a place lives** |
| Specs (`worlds/*.yaml`) | A world's shape: how many people, homes, shops; which profile | Only by naming a profile |
| Worlds' content (laws, `worlds/law/`) | A world's legal code, as records | Yes, as content |

A model's parameters from the literature (Lengnick's Table 1, the Bank of England model's rent share) are rules' parameters, not place facts, and belong with the rule. Where a rule is modelled on a real place's institution (a registry of deaths), the module says so in its doc (docs/behaviour/), not in code.

## A profile

`worlds/profiles/au-vic-2026.json` (a town in regional Victoria, Australia, as of September 2026) was the first; eight more places were written on 2026-10-08. A profile's structure:
- **`values`**: the figures, in the world's units (money in the currency's smallest unit; rates in basis points or per 10,000; configs are integers).
- **`sources`**: a source for every value, keyed by its dotted path (a key covers everything under it). A figure without a source says it's a placeholder, and why.
- **`targets`**: what that place's outcomes look like, with sources: measured against, never set (docs/calibration.md).

A town spec names its profile (`profile: au-vic-2026`); a spec can override a figure for an experiment (wages, households), and that override is the spec's, not the profile's.

**Adding a place:** copy a profile, replace every figure with that place's, with sources; mark what you can't source as a placeholder. Nothing else changes. If a place needs something a profile can't express (a payment that falls as income rises, say), that's a change to the rule (a module parameter or a law's form, documented), made generally.

## A place's laws on pay and support, and when people stop work (2026-10-08)

Three things a profile states as its place's own rules, in forms general enough for any place. Each is published in the town as a law that its office reads; none is a module's setting.

- **Tax on pay** (`public_finance.income_tax.charges`): the law's list of charges, each with what it is charged on, its schedule, its reliefs, and whom it is paid to (`paid_to`: one of `public_finance.funds`, the place's named funds and bodies; `"town"` for a local tax; nothing said: the general government). The forms are in docs/behaviour/public-finance.md. Sums are a year's.
- **When the tax on pay is reported and paid over** (`public_finance.returns`, 2026-10-12): a list of returns, each `{ id, name, charges: [charge ids], every_months (default 1), day (1 to 30), in: "following" (default) | "same" }`: the charges it reports (left out: those no other return names), and the day of the month it falls due by, counted from the first day of the month after the period (`following`) or of the period's last month (`same`). The world's months are 30 days from the year's first day (docs/behaviour/firms.md, "Returns on the law's days"). Left out: every charge monthly on the first day of the month after, a neutral stand-in. The source (`sources["public_finance.returns"]`) marks each day opened, recalled (to confirm) or approximated, as for any figure.
- **Support payments** (`support.payments`): the law's list of payments, each a rule in three parts: who qualifies, how much, for how long; and how often it is paid and from where (`paid_from`: a fund, or the general government). The forms are in docs/behaviour/boundary.md (`world.transfers`). A payment reckoned from a record of contributions needs a charge that is reported worker by worker (`itemised`); the generator refuses a profile that has the one without the other.
- **Stopping work** (`labour_force_share_by_age`): the share of people in the labour force by age band, from the place's labour force survey; the first band is the reference. Each person in the labour force is given their own age of stopping, drawn so the shares by age come out as the survey's (docs/behaviour/citizen.md, "Stopping work"). Left out, everyone stops at `retirement_age`. With it, `retirement_age` is still the age the profile's other figures by age turn on (the retired's savings, a retirement fund's access) and the top of the working ages the measures use; the pension's age is the payment's own, and so is the last age of a payment for those out of work.

**Moving a figure into these forms, and saying a rule truly.** An author states a rule as the law states it where the source gives it; where the source gives only a typical sum or an approximation, the author keeps that as a stand-in and the source note says what the true rule's form would be. Every note that was changed on 2026-10-08 ends with what was moved, what was said for the first time and from what, and what is still a stand-in.

## Work outside a registered standing wage (2026-10-09)

Three things a profile may state under `employment`, each from its place's labour force survey, each left out where no figure was opened (the town is then as it always was: every job registered, on a standing wage, and nobody working for themself). The mechanisms are in docs/behaviour/firms.md ("Jobs an employer does not register", "Work by the day") and entry.md ("A person working for themself").

- **`registered_bps`**, by kind of work (the kinds wages are given for): of employees' jobs, the share the employer registers. State what the survey measures (employees with no social protection, no contract, no work card) and say which.
- **`casual_bps`** and **`day_wage`**, by kind of work: of employees' jobs, the share that is by the day, and a day's pay for it.
- **`own_account`**: `share_bps`, of people in work, those who work for themselves alone; and `kinds`, as the survey divides them by activity, their shares adding to 10,000. Each kind says what it `does`: `"trade"`, `"make"` or `"service"`. **State every kind truly, including those the town cannot hold yet** (it holds trade): the generator places the traders and draws the rest in jobs, and says how many. Do not fold a kind into trade to get its people placed. A trading kind says where it is worked `from` (`"home"` or `"pitch"`) and its `stock_days`.

Stated by four profiles (ke-nakuru-2026, in-tn-2026, br-mg-2026, mx-jal-2026), each note saying what was opened; work by the day by in-tn-2026 alone. Known to be rough, for their authors: each uses one registered share for all three kinds of employer (the sources divide employees by status, not by employer); the kinds of own-account work are national, outside agriculture; the split of traders between a pitch and home is a placeholder; farming and unpaid family workers are left out everywhere. The other five profiles state nothing: their own-account work is 10 to 15% of employment and nearly all registered (to source, per profile).

## What goes wrong in a place, and what holds people there (2026-10-11)

Four things a profile may state for a living town (a spec with `living`); each is read only then, and left out, the town has none of it. The mechanisms are in docs/behaviour/frictions.md and citizen.md ("Things that go wrong, and what follows").

- **`frictions`**: `per_adult_bps_per_year`, of adults, the share who have each kind of problem in a year (`landlord`, `wages`, `bill`, `benefits`, `goods`); `put_right_on_asking_bps`, by matter, how often the other side puts a thing right when asked; the sizes (`pay_short_by_bps`, `bill_over_bps`, `goods_faulty_share_bps`, `repair_hours`); `support` (`wrong_refusal_bps`); `venues` (`answer_days`, `tribunal_days`, `tribunal_fee_hours_of_pay`, `uphold_without_proof_bps`). (Their rates are rows of the world's table of slips, each with the profile's note as its source: foundations F7.) (No profile states how many cases a desk decides in a day, or how long the support office takes over a claim: that is the hours of whoever decides, foundations F6). All nine profiles state the yearly shares from one survey (the World Justice Project's, halved from two years; national), so that places can be compared; each note says which of the rest is opened, which is a placeholder and which is fitted. **State a share of adults, not a rate for those exposed:** the generator turns it into a rate for a let home, a job, a customer or a claim with the town's own counts.
- **`founding`** (read only for a town whose spec turns `living.founding` on; without it such a town is refused): `registered_per_100000_per_year`, how often new associations are registered in the place, with its source, and `informal_multiple`, how many groups are started for each one registered (a placeholder everywhere: no count was found), which code never reads and which together are what `chance_bps` is FITTED to (the chance, at an occasion, that a person of the town's mean temperament and standing founds something); `shared_min`, `rest_days`; `dues_minutes_of_pay` (what a founder may ask a month, in minutes of ordinary pay, the first the plain default), `every_days`, `starts_in_days`, `lasts_minutes`; `lapse_days`, `min_members`, `leave_after_days`; `hall_hire_hours_of_pay`; `fund` and `cause`. Only the first profile's chance is fitted; the others' are that chance scaled by their registered rate, and their notes say so. **State money as time at ordinary pay,** so that no sum is anyone's but the place's own. docs/behaviour/founding.md.
- **`job_move`**: `premium_bps`, `per_year_bps`, `cap_years`, `other_trade_bps`: how much more another job must pay before someone leaves theirs. Stated by au-vic-2026 alone, fitted (its note says to what, and that the fit does not reach the survey's rate, and why).
- **`belief.living_fade_bps_per_month`**: how fast a belief not kept up fades in a living town, fitted to the place's trend in stated affiliation. Stated by au-vic-2026 alone.

**Keep a figure that only a living town reads out of `outlook`:** everything under `outlook` is given to every person of every town, so a new key there moves every town's state (found on 2026-10-11; `orchestrator/test/living-dial.test.ts` catches it).

## The firm and landlord side: ending a job, possession, and business births (2026-10-11)

Four more optional sections, each with its source in the profile and each read as a law or a derived rate, never as a default in code (docs/decisions.md, "Four faults on the firm and landlord side").

- **`audit`** (optional; docs/behaviour/auditor.md): the audit law of the place. `office` (a neutral name), `answer_within_days` (how long a body has to answer the audit office's request for a named item), `report_within_days` (how soon after a period the law says its report is due), and `bodies`: for each of `support` (the office that decides claims for support) and `town_finance` (the town's finance office), the `every_days` of its period, the `checks` (`decisions`: a sample of the period's decisions decided again from the file; `accounts`: its payments in its books against the bank's record) and, for decisions, the `sample` (`share_bps` of the period's decisions, no fewer than `least`, no more than `most`). With `public_service.staff_per_1000.audit` (the audit office's staff). A profile without `audit` has no audit office. Each with its source (`sources["audit.*"]`) or marked a placeholder.

- `employment.termination`: the place's law on ending a job for redundancy (least notice and redundancy pay by service, in days of pay; the small-employer exemption). Published in the town as `law-ending-employment`; employers read it. Every profile's statute is cited and none was opened: to confirm.
- `employment.hours`: the place's law on an employer cutting hours in place of ending jobs: `may_cut` ("notice": by notice; "consent": only with the employee's agreement; "no": not at all, and firms there do not do it), `ordinary_week_hours`, `notice_days`, `floor_bps` (the least hours it may cut to, of the ordinary), `max_days` (the longest spell; 0: none stated), `pay_bps` (the share of pay owed for the hours cut) from `pay_owed_from` employees, and `consent_bps` (of workers asked to agree, the share who do: a placeholder). Published in the town as `law-cutting-hours`. Left out: no such law; firms do not cut hours. Every profile's statute is cited, and what could not be opened is marked to confirm (docs/behaviour/firms.md, "Hours before heads"). `employment.adjustment.hours_step_bps`: the least change in hours worth a notice (placeholder; default 1,000).
- `employment.adjustment`: the months a firm smooths its trade over and what hiring costs in weeks of pay. **Placeholders, the same in all nine profiles**, with a sourced shape (docs/behaviour/firms.md, "Adjusting labour").
- `tenancy.possession`: the place's law on possession for arrears (days of arrears, notice, the tribunal's date, the longest plan, the warrant's days and the notice of the day it is carried out), published as `law-possession` where the town has a tribunal and police; and two shares of how tenants answer (`stay_past_notice_bps`, a placeholder; `leave_on_order_bps`, England's, used everywhere). Victoria's steps were opened; the rest are to confirm or placeholders, named in each profile.
- `tenancy.possession`, more (2026-10-12; docs/behaviour/justice.md, "A tenant's answer"): `answer_bps`, the share of tenants served with an application who answer or attend (opened for Victoria and for Cleveland, Ohio; a named placeholder in the other seven); and what the law says of a plan to pay: `plan_consider` (`may`, `must`, `none`), `plan_barred_rent_months` (where the plan is taken away), `cure_days` (where paying in full within a time ends the claim). Opened for Victoria, England and Germany; recalled for Brazil and Ohio; placeholders for the rest.
- `business.entry_bps_per_year`: the place's business birth rate. The generator derives the monthly chance of starting a business of one from it and the town's own counts. Opened for Australia and the United Kingdom; summaries for the United States, Japan and Mexico; placeholders for Germany, Brazil, India and Kenya.
- `business.trade_credit_days`: the days a supplier ordinarily gives a business to pay for goods delivered; read by the generator for the factories (to the shops that buy from them) and the world market (to the factories that buy inputs). **Placeholder, 30, in two profiles (Victoria and Nakuru), to confirm**; left out of the other seven, whose towns trade for cash with order as before (docs/behaviour/trade.md, "Trade credit").

## Where a town's households live as the year begins (2026-10-12)

Four things a profile states so that a town's first homes follow its households' incomes (docs/behaviour/generator.md, "Homes by income, and payments in force"; docs/decisions.md, "Homes by income"). Each has its source in the profile; a figure not opened says "to confirm" and a figure with no source says PLACEHOLDER and what it stands on.

- **`tenancy.rent_share_of_income`**: `{ of, bands, placeholder? }`. Of the place's renting households, the share in each band of rent over income: `bands` lowest first, each `{ under_bps, bps }`, the shares adding to 10,000 and the last band open (no `under_bps`). **State the bands the office publishes** (two bands, under and over 30%, is a distribution). `of` says what the source divides by: `"gross"` or `"net"` income (the generator then counts pay before or after what is withheld), or `"expenditure"` where no income is surveyed (read as gross income, and said in the note). `"placeholder": true` beside the bands marks bands that were not read (shaped to an opened mean or median, or borrowed), and its source text begins "PLACEHOLDER". It is also a target: the town's own distribution is reported beside it, never fitted to it. Left out: homes are dealt by size alone, as they were.
- **`support.take_up_bps`**: by payment id, of those a payment's rule already entitles as the year begins, the share who have it in payment on the first day. State recipients over the population the rule covers (and say when the base is another age, or the real payment is means-tested and the profile's is not). List only payments the generator can open: a flat sum for age, or for those out of work without a record of contributions. A payment not listed opens with nobody drawing it.
- **`ownership.owner_bps_by_age`**: `[{ from, bps }]`, of households, the share who own their home, by the age of the household's reference person. Read only as how much likelier one age is than another to be among the town's owners (their number is still the houses that `house_owner_bps` makes owner-occupied). Left out: the households needing the most room own, as before.
- **`households.kin_households_most_bps`**, only where the profile states no `extended_bps`: of all households, the share that hold relatives beyond one family (multi-family, multigenerational or extended households, as the census has them). A ceiling for placing people with nothing coming in with kin (docs/behaviour/households.md, "Taken in"); nothing is drawn from it.

As written on 2026-10-12 (a Sonnet 5.5 subagent for each place gathered them; what each opened is in the profile's note): **rent shares** opened as bands for us-oh-2026 (the county's), de-ni-2026 (the state's, of net income) and br-mg-2026 (the one published threshold, national); placeholders around an opened mean or median for au-vic-2026, ke-nakuru-2026 (of expenditure), mx-jal-2026 and gb-eng-2026 (its mean read through a summary); borrowed placeholders for in-tn-2026 and jp-akita-2026 (nothing found). **Take-up**: derived from opened figures for au-vic-2026 (pension), de-ni-2026 (basic income) and jp-akita-2026; to confirm or placeholders elsewhere. **Owners by age**: us-oh-2026, de-ni-2026 and jp-akita-2026 opened in full; au-vic-2026 and gb-eng-2026 in part; br-mg-2026 a proxy by residents' own ages; none for in-tn-2026, ke-nakuru-2026, mx-jal-2026. **Households holding kin**: opened for gb-eng-2026, us-oh-2026, br-mg-2026 and in-tn-2026 (a proxy); to confirm for de-ni-2026; placeholders for au-vic-2026 and ke-nakuru-2026; jp-akita-2026 and mx-jal-2026 state `extended_bps` already.

**Known, and left** ("Imperfect profiles are fine"): ke-nakuru-2026's rents by bedrooms are urban flats' and its wages a market town's: one bedroom is a third of one wage, two bedrooms 71%, a let house 163%. Its town's rent shares sit far above its bands whoever lives where, and its dear houses stand empty at the start. For its author: the dwellings its renters live in (single rooms), or the town's wages.

## What a profile states, and what the generator derives

A profile states what can be observed in a place: wages, rents, prices of real things, rates, shares, ages, how its institutions work. The generator derives the rest by formulas that are the same everywhere. An author never picks a scale.

**Stated:**
- **Pay.** `pay_period_days` (how often wages are paid: 14, 30...) and `wages_per_pay_period` (a full-time gross wage for one period, by kind of work; likewise the doctor's, the teacher's and the officer's). State the place's own period and its own figures; nothing is converted to another place's period.
- **How long work takes** (`values.work_time`, optional; docs/design/foundations.md law 2): `{ person?, system?, sources? }`, seconds a piece of work, by block (`publish`) or by block and what it makes (`publish/<kind of record>`), laid over the generator's neutral placeholders (`WORK_TIME` in `worlds/generator/generate.ts`: minutes for a person, a second for a system, a form to a hearing body and a decision a little longer). `sources` keys are `person.<row>` / `system.<row>`. No profile states any yet (to source: time-use and casework studies, foundations F5/F6).
- **The working week.** `ordinary_hours_per_week` is the week a full-time wage is pay for: what people there work. It is not a legal ceiling above that (a 48-hour maximum is not a 48-hour week). The generator spreads it over the days of the week people work (`round.work_days`), so it has to fit the round's working hours.
- **Shares of output** (`industry`): manufacturing's labour and input shares, retail's purchases and labour shares.
- **Prices of real things**: inputs and building materials by the kilogram, energy and water by their units.
- **What organisations other than firms hold on the first day** (`opening`: the bank, the town, the builder, a landlord) and the least a working adult has saved. These are still stated sums, and they should be ones that make sense against the place's wages.

- **Who lives together** (`households`): the shares of households by kind, as the place's census counts them, and, where the census has them, extended households: their share (`extended_bps`), the family they are built around and which relatives live with it (docs/behaviour/households.md). Do not fold extended households into another kind: state them, or leave them out and say so.
- **How people there judge a home's size** (`households.occupancy`): the place's own occupancy standard, where it has one (the most people to a bedroom, the age from which someone has a room of their own, the age under which a boy and a girl share, whether the living room is slept in). Left out, the standard statistics offices commonly apply is used, which is one country's.
- **How many people a home can physically hold** (`homes`: bodies for each bedroom, and besides): it stands for floor area. Left out, two and two.

- **How people there start out in what they trust** (`outlook`, optional; docs/behaviour/citizen.md, "The record"): `trust`, the share with confidence in each kind of institution the place's survey asks about (`government`, `policing`, `courts`, `civil_service`, `banks`, `press`, `unions`, `religious`, `companies`: whichever it gives); `people_bps`, the share who say most people can be trusted; `agency` (`mean_bps`, `sd_bps`), how much control over their lives people feel. Whatever is left out starts from the citizen's own placeholder, and the note says so. All nine profiles state the first two from the World Values Survey's wave 7, nationally; none states `agency` (it needs the survey's microdata).
- **How long people have lived where they live and worked where they work** (`outlook.residence`, `outlook.job_tenure`, and `outlook.group_years` only if a source exists; optional; docs/behaviour/citizen.md): each is `{ "bands": [ { "under_years": 1, "bps": N }, { "under_years": 5, "bps": N }, ..., { "bps": N } ] }`: shares of adults (residence: at their address or locality; job_tenure: the employed, with their employer or in their work; group_years: members, in their group) in basis points summing to 10000, `under_years` strictly increasing, the last band open (no `under_years`). `"placeholder": true` beside the bands marks a borrowed figure, and its source text (the `sources` keys `outlook.residence`, `outlook.job_tenure`) begins "PLACEHOLDER:" and says what it was borrowed from. The generator uses them only for a town whose spec has a `living` section (`living: {}`; off with `starting_attachment: false`): each housed adult gets `started.place_days`, each employed or self-employed adult `started.trade_days`, and each adult in a group `started.group_days`, drawn from the bands on a stream of its own (`rng("living")`) and capped by the person's age (residence) or their age less 15 (tenure); the open last band is read as from its lower bound to three times it. Every citizen also gets `attach_mean: { place_bps, trade_bps }`, the town's mean attachment under the citizen's default half-lives (5 and 3 years) over the bands' midpoints (the open band's taken as twice its lower bound). A spec without `living` is generated exactly as before. As of 2026-10-11: job tenure is the OECD's "Employment by job tenure intervals" for Australia, Germany, Great Britain, Japan, Mexico and Brazil, and the BLS's Employee Tenure table for the United States (all national); India and Kenya are placeholders borrowing Brazil's; residence is Australia's own (ABS Census 2021, national, built from the one-year and five-year mobility rates) and a placeholder borrowing it everywhere else (to source: each place's own years-since-moving-in tables).
- **How often asking gets a yes** (`outlook.asked`; optional; docs/behaviour/firm-wants.md): `rise_granted_bps`, of employees who asked their employer for a pay rise, the share who got one. The generator gives it to each firm and landlord of a town whose spec has `living` as where its own practice starts (`org.rise_bps`); a profile without it leaves the module's placeholder. The first profile has it (Artz, Goodall and Oswald 2018); the others do not yet.

**Derived (don't state them):**
- **An hour's pay**: a period's pay over the hours worked in it (the working week, by the pay period).
- **An item of goods.** Goods are counted in items, the town's own unit and no real thing: by default an item is what half an hour of factory work makes, so its price at the factory gate is that work's pay over manufacturing's labour share. Its time to make, its inputs, the shop's price and the time to serve it follow from the profile's shares. The unit's size changes no outcome (tested: twice the price is half the items, the same money and the same staff). `prices.goods_world` may be stated to fix the unit (the first profile's item is 100.00); the generator then checks it is a size whole numbers can carry (an item's seconds of work and units of inputs within 5% of what the shares give, and no more than a working day's work) and refuses the profile if not, saying so.
- **A firm's opening money**: its working capital. A shop's is a month's stock and three months' wages for its opening staff; a factory's is a month's wages and a month's inputs for its opening staff. A profile that states `opening.shop` or `opening.factory` is refused. (Until 2026-10-08 a factory's was a stated sum, and whether a town worked turned on how that sum happened to compare with half a month of its factories' inputs: see the decisions log, "A town from any place".)
- **What the town's shops expect to sell**, their opening staff and stock, and the world market's demand for the factories' goods: from wages after tax, pensions by how often they are paid, and the rent of the homes that are let.

## Sums and figures in a town's laws (2026-10-09)

The example legal code (`worlds/law/`) holds no sum of money and no place's figure. A law whose substance is figures is written from a schedule with the profile's own (rent increases, industrial action, ending a tenancy: `tenancy.end_notice_days`, `tenancy.tenant_notice_days`). A sum a law names in free words stands in the code as `{name}` and the generator writes it in from the profile, as the town writes its money: the planning law's fine is `town_finance.unpermitted_building_fine`, which the building inspector is given too. All three are placeholders in every profile today, and say so. `orchestrator/test/generality.test.ts` holds the code to no digit, no sign and no currency word, and every generated town's words to its own money.

## What a place may not have

A place that has no such institution leaves the figure out. Nothing is stated as nought to stand for "none":
- no payment to the unemployed: leave it out of `support.payments` (nobody claims one, none is paid or counted); no payments at all: an empty list, and no support law is written;
- no tax on pay: an empty list of charges (no law is written, nothing is withheld);
- no landfill charge: leave out `services.landfill_levy_per_tonne` (no budget line for it);
- no serviceability buffer: leave out `credit.serviceability_buffer_bps`;
- no rule on rent increases: leave out `tenancy.rent_review_months` and `rent_increase_notice_days` (no such law is written; `tenancy.rent_review_practice_months` says how often landlords review rents anyway, if they do);
- no ballot before industrial action, or no notice of it, or neither: leave out whichever of `industrial_action.ballot_turnout_bps`, `ballot_pass_bps` and `notice_days` the place does not require. The law says what is stated and nothing of the rest (a ballot's turnout alone, its majority alone, the notice alone, or any of them together), and with none of the three there is no such law. The same for the two figures of the law on rent increases and the two of the law on ending a tenancy;
- no interest on overdue rates: leave out `public_finance.rates_penalty_interest_bps` (the rating law has no "or else");
- no retirement fund: leave out `retirement_fund` (and a town doesn't ask for one);
- no informal work to speak of: leave out `employment` (or the part of it that does not apply);
- voting required by law or not: `elections.compulsory_voting` (the town's election law says "must" or "may").

Still required, though some places lack them (to do): a union, council rates, a clinic and school funded from outside.

## Guardrails

- **`orchestrator/test/generality.test.ts`:**
  - platform code (kernel, participants, orchestrator, translator, measure, the generator, contracts) must not name a place, its statistics offices, agencies, payments or currency;
  - platform code (kernel, participants, orchestrator, translator, measure) must not write a currency's sign or its words: a world's money is written as its config says (`world.currencies`: its sign, its words, its decimal places), which a profile supplies. The test lists what it leaves alone, and why. The translator's instructions are among what it checks (they name no currency and are the same for every world: the translator copies an amount as written, and the parser, which has the world's currency, reads it);
  - every figure in every profile must have a source;
  - a town must generate from every profile, taking its figures from it;
  - platform code must not name any of a generated world's entities, accounts or buildings: a module is told whom it deals with (its parameters), and `need()` fails at once if a world doesn't say.
- **CLAUDE.md** states the rule.
- **Reviews:** a new place-specific figure goes in a profile, with its source, or it doesn't go in.

## Known exceptions (to fix)

- **A place says how it writes money** (done 2026-10-08; docs/decisions.md, "A place states how it writes money"). A currency's block may say `decimal_mark`, `group_mark`, `grouping` (`[3]` or `[3, 2]`), `symbol_after`, `symbol_spaced` and `number_words`, each optional and absent meaning what a block without them always meant (a point, commas in threes, the sign first with no space; the code, being letters, set off by a space). `formatMoney` and the resolver's `format_money` write an amount that way; `parseAmount` and `parse_amount` use the fields to settle what a mark alone cannot ("1.234" in a place whose decimal mark is a comma is one thousand two hundred and thirty-four), and still refuse what stays ambiguous (a point once with three digits after it where the block says neither its decimal mark nor that a point groups digits). Lakh and crore are the Indian profile's `number_words`: a block that does not list them does not get them. Each profile states its fields with a source in its `sources.currency` (Unicode CLDR for every place, with a style guide where one was opened); a profile whose place writes money as the defaults do may still state `decimal_mark` and `group_mark`, which is what lets "$1.234" be read as a decimal with a digit too many instead of being refused as two numbers. The training data's invented currencies are the same fields (translator/train/data.ts, `WrittenCurrency`). Not covered: a place's words for large numbers written in its own language (Portuguese *mil*, Japanese *man*): `number_words` would hold them, but no source for how they appear in English text was opened, so no profile lists any.
- **Other signs and abbreviations a place uses for its money** ("Rs", "A$", "p") are read only if the profile lists them among `unit_words`, which takes letters alone.
- **The finance officer's module still has a default for the payment its procedure needs approval over** (`approval_over`); the generated town now gives it from the profile, but the default should go. The same for the building inspector's `fine`.
- **Names:** the helper that words an amount is still called `usd`, and an amount in the smallest unit `cents`, across the behaviour modules (about 400 uses). They write whatever the world's currency is; the names are one place's. Not renamed on 2026-10-08, when the measures' money columns were (`money_in`, `money_out`, `paid_to_town`, in the world's own currency by `runs.minor_units`): several branches were editing those modules at once. A mechanical rename for a quiet moment.
- **Sums rounded to a figure that suits a currency with two decimal places and wages in the thousands:** a firm asks its bank for working capital to the nearest 100,000 of the smallest unit, a household for 10,000 (firm.producer, firm.retailer, citizen.default). Harmless where amounts are larger; coarse where a wage is a few thousand of the smallest unit. To derive from the wage.
- **Module defaults in money and in items** (a wage of 150,000, a price of 6,000, essentials of 40,000, a wholesale order of at least 50 items, a household's fortnight of 10 items for how well subsistence is met): placeholders the generated town overrides, except the last two, which are counts of items and so depend on the unit's size where a profile fixes it.
- **Shopping is fortnightly** (citizen.default buys every 14 days) and a job's notice is 14 days, whatever the pay period.
- **The profiles' own notes on `opening`** still mention a shop's and a factory's opening sums (removed from the figures on 2026-10-08; the prose is their authors').
- **Extended households are stated by two profiles only** (`jp-akita-2026`, `mx-jal-2026`: the two whose notes give the share; 2026-10-08). `in-tn-2026` and `ke-nakuru-2026` say they are common and give no share for them (the first folds them into `group_bps`, a placeholder): for their authors, with the census table by household type. No profile states its place's own occupancy standard or a home's physical room: every town judges crowding by the first profile's standard and holds two bodies a bedroom and two besides. For the Kenyan and Indian towns in particular these are not their places' (docs/design/profile-depth.md, item 2): for their authors.
- **Akita's children.** Its town has 249 people under 18 in 1,000 where its census has 97, with or without extended households: the profile's children-at-home figures are placeholders (a child at home is any age to 24, evenly), and most children living with parents there are grown. For its author.
- **Three profiles state a working week longer than their daily round** (gb-eng-2026: 48 hours over five days of 9 to 5; mx-jal-2026: 48 over five; ke-nakuru-2026: 52 over five days of 8 to 5): each gave the legal ceiling. The town generates and runs (a wage buys the hours stated), but the day is longer than the round shows. For their authors.
- **No figure of nought stands for "none" in any profile** (2026-10-09; docs/decisions.md, "A rule a place does not have is absent from its law"). Left out, each because its profile's own note says the place has no such rule: the landfill charge (de-ni-2026, in-tn-2026, ke-nakuru-2026); the serviceability buffer (us-oh-2026, de-ni-2026, in-tn-2026, mx-jal-2026); a ballot's turnout (de-ni-2026) and both ballot figures (in-tn-2026); the notice of industrial action (us-oh-2026, de-ni-2026); interest on overdue rates (gb-eng-2026). No other figure was changed. Two things this leaves for the authors: de-ni-2026 still states a ballot majority of 75%, which its note says is the unions' own rule and not statute, so its town's law requires that majority and nothing else; us-oh-2026's ballot figures are placeholders its note calls union practice, so its town's law is a ballot and no notice. A figure stated as nought is in any case read as not stated by the laws made from figures, and `orchestrator/test/places.test.ts` holds the profiles to none.
- **Every profile states `pay_period_days` 14**, with wages its author converted to fourteen days: where pay is monthly, the author restates the wages for a month and sets 30.
- **Stated sums that should follow from wages** and still don't: what the bank, the town, the builder and a landlord hold on the first day, the least a working adult has saved, and the retired's savings (`opening`, `retiree_savings`). Each author scaled the first profile's by a wage ratio.
- **Some rules were modelled on one country's institutions:**
  - the stand-ins that remain in the profiles' support payments and taxes (each note says which): a family transfer paid as a flat payment to those out of work (br-mg-2026), a means-tested pension paid to everyone (in-tn-2026), tariffs approximated by steps (de-ni-2026, jp-akita-2026, br-mg-2026);
  - the example legal code's figures, now in the profiles as placeholders carried over from the first (the notices to end a tenancy; the fine for building without a permit), and its forms (a law of succession to partner, adult children, town; a planning permit for every building).
  - the retirement fund's rules, in a world that has one (`fund.retirement`, off by default: a pension of at least a yearly share of the balance, rising with age; paid from the age people here stop work), after Australia's account-based pensions.

  The rules are general in form, but they were chosen with one place in mind. As a second profile is added, each should be checked.
- **A grant that is a whole municipality's general transfer** (2026-10-09; docs/decisions.md, "A grant several times the town's whole budget"). `public_finance.grants_per_resident_per_year` in `jp-akita-2026` and `br-mg-2026` is the real town's whole general-purpose transfer for each resident, which there pays for everything it runs; the generated town runs waste collection, upkeep, a park and roads, and a wildlife survey, so its opening budget spends about an eighth of what the grant pays in (7.4 and 8.5 times the budget at 1,000 people). `ke-nakuru-2026` and `mx-jal-2026` are placeholders sized the same way (2.2 and 1.9 times). Each profile's own note on the figure is flagged. For their authors: the grant for the functions the town has, or the source's spending by purpose so that the share can be stated. A test holds that a profile whose grant is more than one and a half times its town's whole opening budget says so in that note (`orchestrator/test/places.test.ts`). `au-vic-2026`'s note on the same figure still says local roads grants are left out because "the town has no roads yet": the town has a roads line now.


## What a town holds in common, and where people come from (2026-10-13)

`values.culture` is optional, read only for a living town that turns the culture dial on (docs/behaviour/culture.md). It is not in `outlook`, which every town is given.
- **`conducts`**: for each conduct the build lists, `does_bps` (the share who do it) and `approves_bps` (the share who approve), each with its source in `sources["culture.conducts"]`. A conduct with no figure runs on its own rate in the profile and a marked placeholder for approval.
- **`origin`**: where people come from, as groups the place's own statistics name: `groups` (`id`, `name`, `share_bps`, summing to 10000), `same_household_bps` (of two adults living together, how often both are of one group), `homophily_bps` (how much likelier a tie is within a group, as a share of chance), and optionally `by_group` (a group's figures for a conduct, replacing the place's). Its source (`sources["culture.origin"]`) says which table, what year, whether it was opened or is "(to confirm)", and what it says of children of mixed couples. **A profile with no `origin` has no such groups.** No place's figure or group name is ever in platform code.
