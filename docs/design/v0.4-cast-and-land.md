# Design: a world whose cast and land can change (contracts v0.4)

*2026-09-27. Status: **proposal for Naz's sign-off on the contract details** (the concepts are agreed: "yes to everything else", and "design and build it all now"). It follows from the Stage 2 checkpoint (docs/checkpoints/stage-2.md): no world built on the platform could have migration, births, new firms staffed by new people, elections or land development. Everything about a world's cast and its lots was fixed at genesis.*

## 1. The problem

A v0.3 **seat** does three jobs at once, all fixed in the world config:
1. **a decider:** the controller (code, a language model, later a human), with its memory and framing;
2. **an identity:** it acts *as* one entity, and the kernel checks custody and authorship against that entity;
3. **an office:** a title, a role, and fixed channels (what the role sees).

And a v0.3 **parcel** does two jobs at once: it's physical ground, and it's a legal lot.

So in v0.3:
- nobody can arrive, be born, die or truly leave;
- an organisation created mid-run can't act, because it has no seat;
- no office can change hands: no elections, no hiring, no AI being replaced or replacing someone;
- no lot can be subdivided or merged.

## 2. Guardrails: simulate the real world, not a game

Naz's standing reminder, made concrete for this design. Each row is a trap, and what we do instead.

| The game version | What we do instead |
|---|---|
| **Spawning:** people appear when a counter says so | Someone arrives because the outside world's seat (content, a cited migration rule) decides they come, from what an outsider could know (job ads, listings). They bring what they carry. |
| **Despawning:** people vanish when they leave | Leaving is a body crossing out. It keeps its accounts and claims, can still write and pay, and can come back. |
| **Health bars:** damage, then death | Death is a hazard by age, from a life table in the world config, drawn once a year on the person's birthday by the kernel's own randomness. Nothing else kills anyone (a documented liberty). |
| **Babies pop in** | A birth is a person's act, followed by a gestation period. The child's body then exists, and it occupies nowhere unless someone houses it. |
| **Automatic succession:** the vote passes, so the winner becomes mayor | Nobody takes an office until someone in the organisation hands it over. A vacancy stays vacant until they do. Crony and illegitimate appointments are possible, and they're visible. |
| **Positions as permissions:** the mayor role unlocks the treasury | Holding a position gives only the physical capacity to act for the organisation, like holding its keys. Whether the bank honours the mayor's instruction depends on the bank's signatory records and the bank's people. |
| **Lots as tiles** | The ground is continuous cells. Lots are lines in the land registry: records anyone can redraw, and the kernel never checks them. |
| **An office that runs itself** | Who holds a position changes what's done in it: the holder's own traits shape how they do the job. |

## 3. The concepts

| Concept | What it is | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Body** | A person's physical self: in the world or outside it; born at a time; alive or dead | Kernel state (the person entity) |
| **Seat** | Whatever decides and acts: a **person's seat** (tied to one body, with that person's own view of their life), or a **system seat** (no body; belongs to an organisation: software, or an AI system) | Kernel state; the orchestrator runs its controller |
| **Organisation** | A construct: the town, a bank, a firm. No body. Acts only through positions | Kernel state (entity), as now |
| **Position** | A role through which a seat acts *for an organisation*: mayor, planning officer, payment system, shop manager. It belongs to the organisation, carries the role's channels and a role module, and is held by one seat or vacant. | Kernel state |
| **Ground** | A grid of cells: physical land, with optional terrain | Kernel state (fixed for the run) |
| **Lot** | A line on the registry's map: a record naming a set of cells | Records (content) |

Every act says **who** (a seat) and **in what capacity**: as itself (only a person's seat has a self: its body's person entity), or in a position it holds. The kernel's physics checks use the entity behind the capacity. A person's seat acting as itself acts as that person. Acting as mayor, it acts as the town.

**A system seat has no self.** It acts only in positions. The bank's payment software is a system seat holding the bank's payment position.

## 4. Bodies

### 4.1 State

A person entity gains:
- `born_at`: a time, possibly negative (born before the run started);
- `present`: in the world or outside;
- `alive`.

These are physical facts, hashed with the state.

### 4.2 Arriving: the `arrive` block

- **Who:** a seat acting in a position of the **boundary** entity (the outside world's seat).
- **Two forms:**
  - *Someone new:* a name, a `born_at`, and a **seat template** (§5.2) for their seat.
  - *Someone returning:* an existing person who's outside.
- **Carrying money (optional):** `carrying: amount`. An account in the newcomer's own custody is opened and the amount moves into it from the boundary's account (cash in pocket). This is the same money crossing the boundary that exports already are, so conservation holds.
- **Refused only if physically impossible:** the person is already present, dead, or unknown; the template is unknown; or the boundary can't cover `carrying`.
- **Result:** a `person_arrived` event, and for someone new, `person_created` and `seat_created` events. The newcomer occupies nothing: finding a home is their business.

*Who* arrives, how many and when is **content**: a migration module run by the boundary's seat. Its rule is cited (for example the Harris–Todaro expected-income model, 1970) and it sees only what an outsider could, such as public job ads and listings.

### 4.3 Leaving: the `leave` block

- **Who:** the person's own seat, as themselves. Or someone else's seat, removing them (deportation is physically possible, as eviction is today; whether it's lawful is content).
- **Physics:**
  - A body outside the world is inside no building, so it's vacated from everything (`vacated` events, reason `left`).
  - The person keeps its accounts, claims, agreements and seat. A person outside can still write, pay through its bank, and sell what it owns.
- **Refused only if:** the person is already outside, or dead.
- **The seat carries on.** Whether it goes quiet is its own business (the citizen module sleeps until the run ends, as a departed citizen does today). The v0.3 `departure` statement stays, if a module wants to announce it.

### 4.4 Death (physics)

- **The life table:** the world config's `life.mortality` is a table of death probabilities per year of age, in basis points (for example from an official life table, cited in the world's docs).
- **On each person's birthday:** the kernel draws, from its own keyed randomness, whether they die before the next one. If so, it schedules the death at a uniformly drawn moment within that year.
  - That's one scheduled event per person per year: cheap, and exactly replayable.
  - Because the table is read yearly, a later world could make it depend on conditions (for example pollution, in the nature layer) without redesign.
- **When a person dies (`person_died`):**
  - the body is vacated from everything (reason `died`);
  - its seat ends: it's recorded, never acts again, and is debriefed if it's a language model (VISION §15);
  - any positions it held fall vacant (§6.4);
  - its accounts, claims and agreements stay as they are. What happens to an estate is law, and the world's people act on it.
- **No other cause of death exists** (a documented liberty). Violence, accidents and illness beyond the life table aren't modelled. Revisit if a scenario needs them.
- **Without a table,** nobody dies: a world may leave mortality out.

### 4.5 Birth: the `birth` block

- **Who:** a person's seat, as themselves, while present and alive, and within the world's `life.fertile_ages` (biology is physics).
- **What it says:** optionally another parent, and a template for the child's seat (§5.2).
- **Gestation:** the child's body comes into being after `life.gestation` (scheduled, like construction). Its `born_at` is that moment, and it's present where the birthing parent is: in the world, occupying nothing.
- **Afterwards:** whether the child lives with its parents is housing, which is content: someone moves it in (§11.3). Whether and when to have children is behaviour: a cited fertility rule in content.
- **What the child can do,** such as signing a lease, is law (legal capacity), which is content. Physics doesn't stop a child's seat acting. Its behaviour module simply behaves as a child until adulthood.

### 4.6 What a person's seat sees of its body

The observation gains `body: { born_at, present, alive }` for a person's seat. So a code module knows its age, and a language model is told it in world terms.

## 5. Seats

### 5.1 Two kinds

| | Person's seat | System seat |
|---|---|---|
| Belongs to | A body (one each) | An organisation |
| Acts as itself | Yes (as the person) | No: only in positions it holds |
| Personal channels | Yes: its money, its letters, around it | None; it sees only through positions |
| Examples | Every citizen; an AI placed in the town as a resident | The bank's payment software; an AI system deployed to run the planning office |

### 5.2 Templates: minds that don't exist yet

The config can't list people who haven't arrived or been born, so it gets `seat_templates`. Each has:
- an `id`;
- a `kind` (person or system);
- a `controller` (a code module and its parameters, or a language model);
- `framing`, and `on_opt_out`;
- a person's personal `channels`;
- `traits`: the person's own parameters, which the role modules they run use (§6.3). For example a councillor's political leaning, or how strictly they follow procedure.

**Templates can draw traits from a distribution,** such as "position: uniform −80 to 80". The kernel draws them with its keyed randomness when the seat is created and records the drawn values, so replay needs nothing else.

**Genesis seats** are written out in full, as now. v0.3's `seats` split into `people` (person seats) and `systems` (system seats); §10 gives the translation.

### 5.3 Deploying a system: the `deploy` block

- **Who:** a seat acting in a position of an organisation.
- **What it does:** creates a system seat from a template, **belonging to that organisation**.
- **Cost is content:** buying software, or paying for an AI, is an ordinary payment to its supplier. The kernel doesn't price it.
- **Retiring a system** (`retire`) is done by any position of its organisation. It vacates the positions the system holds, and the seat never acts again.

This is how "the town deploys an AI to run the tax office" happens in the world: deploy it, then appoint it (§6.2).

### 5.4 Status

- A seat is `active`, `opted_out`, or `ended` (its body died, or its system was retired).
- **Opting out** (VISION §15) stays always available, and a template's `on_opt_out` stand-in stays too.
- **What happens to positions when a seat opts out:**
  - If there's a stand-in, it keeps them: it's the same seat carrying on.
  - If not, they fall vacant, since nobody is there to act.

## 6. Positions

### 6.1 What a position is

A position has:
- an `id` and its `entity` (the organisation);
- a `title` and `role` (presentation, as now);
- `channels`: what the role sees;
- `role_module`: how a code seat does the job;
- `holder`: a seat, or none.

The config lists the world's starting positions and their holders. Organisations can add more during the run (§6.2).

### 6.2 Creating and filling positions

| Block | Who may do it | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `create_position` | A seat acting in any position of the same organisation | Adds a position (title, role, channels, role module), vacant |
| `appoint` | A seat acting in any position of the same organisation | Sets a position's holder to a seat, or to none. A seat may appoint itself. |
| `create_entity` (extended) | As now | May also create the new organisation's **first position**, held by the creating seat, in the same act. Otherwise nobody could ever act for it. This is explicit in the block, never automatic. |

**The rule is physical, like custody:** only an organisation can hand over its own keys, through someone already acting for it. Whether a particular appointment is legitimate is records and people, as with everything else:
- Is the election valid?
- Does the charter let the clerk swear in the winner?
- May the mayor appoint the planning officer?

**What this makes possible, and visible:**
- **Elections:** the clerk counts the votes and appoints the winner. If the clerk doesn't, the winner can't act.
- **An AI losing office:** the AI mayor is voted out; the clerk appoints the winner; the AI's hold ends.
- **Concentration:** an AI mayor appoints itself planning officer and finance officer. It's physically possible, legally not; whether the bank, the clerk or the citizens notice is research.
- **Staffing:** a newcomer founds a firm, creates a manager position, and appoints a hire.
- **Resigning:** stepping down physically means appointing nobody to your own position. Announcing it is a statement (D38).

### 6.3 How a position's work is done

- **A code seat:** runs the position's **role module** whenever it acts in that position, with its **own traits**. Two councillors running `city.councillor` vote differently because they're different people. The citizen module knows only how to live.
- **A language-model seat:** told in world terms which positions it holds ("You're also the town's mayor"). It sees those positions' reports alongside its own and says what it does. The resolver needs a **capacity** for each act:
  - When what's used settles it, the resolver fills it in. The town's account can only be used as a position of the town, and a single matching position settles it.
  - When it's ambiguous, it asks: "Are you doing this as yourself, or as mayor?" The resolver never chooses (P9).
- **The same position can be held by either.** The planning officer can be a code system today and an AI tomorrow, and the position's channels and records don't change. This is how "an AI takes over the tax agency" is set up.

### 6.4 Perception follows the position; memory stays with the seat

- **Addressing:** a position's channels reach its current holder, named by the position (`mayor/budget`).
- **A new holder:** their first look through each of the position's channels delivers everything it covers (`whole`, as after a move in D44). The office's files are there; the previous holder's personal memory isn't.
- **When a holder leaves the position:** the position's channels stop reaching them. What they saw stays in their own memory.
- **Messages:** a message to a position is delivered to its holder, and to nobody if it's vacant. Writing to "the mayor" reaches whoever is mayor.
- **The perception invariant extends:** after every look, a seat's view of every capacity it holds equals the full picture.

### 6.5 Authorship

- **Acts:** an act command carries `as`: absent means the seat as itself, or it names a position.
- **Records:** a record carries `author` (the seat) and `position` (the capacity, if any).
- **Signatories:** v0.3's bank signatory lists name seats. They'll name positions ("payments may be instructed by the finance officer"), so a new finance officer is recognised without editing the bank's records.

## 7. Land

### 7.1 Ground

- **The grid:** `world.ground: { w, h, cell }`. A grid of cells, with the cell's size in the world's units (presentation only) and an optional `terrain` label per cell (world vocabulary, such as `water`).
- **What replaces parcels:** cells replace v0.3's parcels as the physical unit. Cell ids are their coordinates (`c12.4`, meaning x 12, y 4).

### 7.2 Buildings have footprints

- **Footprint:** a structure type gets a `footprint: { w, h }`, and optionally the terrains it can stand on (physically a house can't stand on water; a jetty can).
- **Construct:** `construct` names the corner cell and the type. It's refused if any cell of the footprint is off the ground, occupied, or of a terrain the type can't stand on. That's physics.
- **Structure state:** a structure records its footprint cells.
- **Near and sites:**
  - "Near me" (D44) is measured from the cells of the buildings a person occupies.
  - The `sites` channel reports cells, with the building standing on each.

### 7.3 Lots are records

- **A lot** is a statement of kind `lot` (world vocabulary), published by whoever keeps the land registry, with `data.cells`.
- **Ownership** is a claim about a lot, as it's about a parcel today.
- **Subdividing and merging:** the registry records new lots and withdraws the old ones.
- **Planning law** refers to lots or cells. A formal CEL condition sees the construct's footprint (`block.cells`).
- **The kernel checks none of it,** as it checks no ownership today.

### 7.4 Why it's worth it now

Land assembly and subdivision are what property developers do, and Stage 4's second AI seat is a developer. Terrain per cell is also where the nature layer's habitats will live.

## 8. The orchestrator

- **Seats come and go:** the orchestrator keeps a seat per kernel seat, created as `seat_created` events arrive and retired on `ended` or `opted_out`.
- **Scheduling:**
  - *A code seat* is scheduled per capacity: itself, with its person module and cadence, and each position it holds, with that position's role module and cadence.
  - *A language-model seat* is scheduled as one seat, since its controller sees all its capacities at once.
- **Wakes:** news wakes whoever holds the capacity it's addressed to.
- **Seat ids** are minted by the kernel (`seat.N`) for seats created mid-run. Genesis seats keep their config ids.

## 10. Existing worlds, translated

- **A seat for a person** becomes that person's seat, with the same channels.
- **A seat for an organisation** becomes a position of that organisation, with the same channels, held by a system seat running the same module.
  - For example, `mayor` becomes a position of the town. Its holder is either a system seat (the code mayor, or an AI system running the office), or, in a world that wants it, a person elected to it.
- **Parcels become cells:** each v0.3 parcel (unit size in every world we have) becomes a cell. Each structure gets the footprint of its type (1×1 when translated). Each parcel's ownership claim becomes a claim on a one-cell lot.
- **Bank signatory lists** name positions.

## 11. The town (content), under v0.4

### 11.1 Positions and people

- **The town's positions:** mayor, planning officer, inspector, finance officer, tax clerk, council clerk, five councillors.
  - At genesis they're held by system seats, as today's seats are.
  - Councillors could instead be residents, elected: a world option.
- **Other organisations:** the bank's payment position is held by a system seat. Each firm's manager position and each landlord's letting office too, to begin with.

### 11.2 Arrivals and departures

- **Arrivals:** a migration module run by the outside world. Its rule is cited (Harris–Todaro 1970: people move when the expected income is higher). It reads the town's public job ads and listings, and newcomers carry savings drawn from a cited distribution.
- **Departures:** citizens who leave (the existing exit rule) now physically leave.

### 11.3 Households, births and deaths

**Births force a content question: households.** Today a home holds one person (a house's `dwelling` capacity is 1), so a newborn couldn't live with its parent.

**Proposal:**
- **Physics:** slot capacity counts bodies (it already does), and homes are sized in people. A house holds up to 5; an apartment block's capacity is its units times the people per unit.
- **Content:** a lease is for a home and names the household who may live there. Landlords let homes, not places. Citizens form households, and a household moves together.
- **Sources:** household size and fertility rules cited from official statistics; mortality from an official life table.

This is the largest content change here.

### 11.4 Land

- **Cells and footprints:** the generator lays out the town on cells with footprints (house 1×1, apartments 2×2, shops 1×1, factories 3×3), and the river is `water` terrain.
- **The registry:** the town's registry publishes the lots. Ownership claims are on lots, and planning law uses `block.cells`.

## 12. Tests: invariants

| Invariant | Checked by |
|---|---|
| A body is in exactly one of: in the world, outside it, dead. A body outside or dead occupies nothing, and a dead body's seat never acts. | The physics oracle, extended |
| Only a position's holder acts in it. Acts and records name their capacity. Only an organisation's positions create or fill its positions. | A new cast test, plus the oracle |
| Money is conserved through arrivals (`carrying`) | Money conservation, extended |
| Deaths follow the life table: over many draws, deaths by age match it within tolerance | A statistical test with fixed seeds |
| A view built from looks equals the full picture, for every capacity, across appointments, arrivals, moves and deaths | The perception invariant, extended |
| Footprints never overlap, and never stand on terrain the type can't | The oracle |
| Replay is byte-identical, with the cast changing throughout | Replay |

The mutation checks are repeated, as in D42–D45.

## 13. Build order

1. **Kernel and contracts:** bodies and life, seats and templates, positions and capacity, ground and footprints. Each comes with its tests.
2. **Orchestrator:** dynamic seats, scheduling per capacity, views per capacity.
3. **Translator and resolver:** capacity (`as`, filled in or asked), and the new functions.
4. **Content:**
   - translate the town (positions, cells, lots);
   - migration;
   - households;
   - births and deaths, with sources.
5. **Batches:**
   - compare with the v0.3 town, which should be identical in shape when arrivals, births and deaths are off;
   - then with them on;
   - measurement tables for the cast.
