# Design proposal: a physics kernel, with every human construct as data (contracts v0.2)

*Status: **SUPERSEDED IN PART by D19** (2026-09-25): laws don't block or execute anything; entities act. §6.2–6.3's `forbid`, `act` and `adjust` effects, and §7–8's use of them, are withdrawn. Being rewritten. Nothing here is built.*

*Original status: proposal for Naz's review. It replaces the parts of contracts v0.1 that put law in the kernel (see [the audit](../audit-kernel-law.md) and decision D18).*

---

## 1. The principle

> "Equate the kernel with physics, and that's about it. Anything else that's a human construct is expressed through it." (Naz)

The kernel knows physics and provides a few **generic mechanisms**. Every human construct is **data**: laws, property, ownership (including ownership of money), leases, jobs, organisations, jurisdiction. It's written in a world's config and can be changed in-world by seats through blocks.

The loop is unchanged. A seat speaks plain language, the translator compiles it into blocks, the seat signs off, and the kernel validates and applies. Laws are one more thing the translator can produce.

## 2. Three layers

| Layer | What's in it | Who defines it |
|---|---|---|
| **Physics** | Time; money (conserved, integer, can't go below zero); land (finite parcels); structures (occupy a parcel, cost time and money to build, have physical capacity); bodies (persons: they exist, occupy space, can't be conjured by a block); the world's edge (boundary) | The kernel. Fixed. |
| **Generic mechanisms** | Entities, claims, agreements, rules, mandates, voice. Machinery with no content: the kernel knows *how* a claim is recorded or a rule is evaluated, never *what* any particular claim or rule means | The kernel. Fixed. |
| **Constructs** | Property law, tenancy law, planning law, corporate law, the constitution (who may make laws); the vocabulary of claim kinds ("surface", "use", "control"), entity kinds ("firm", "trust") and agreement templates ("lease", "employment") | World config, as data. Changeable in-world. |

**The default is anarchy.** A world with no rules has no ownership: any seat can build on any parcel or spend any entity's money. That follows from the principle, and it was accepted explicitly. A world gets order by including laws. We ship an **example legal code** (`worlds/law/`) as data that a world includes deliberately; it is an example, not a default.

## 3. Blocks in v0.2

Seventeen blocks. The kernel checks **only physics and mechanics** for each; everything else is up to rules.

| Block | Kind | What the kernel checks (physics and mechanics only) |
|---|---|---|
| `transfer` | physics | Funds exist; no overflow; the boundary can't pay |
| `construct` | physics | Parcel exists and is empty; catalogue type exists; payer can pay the catalogue cost to the contractor; completes after the build time. **No ownership is assigned.** The kernel records only `built_by` (who paid), a historical fact. |
| `demolish` | physics | Structure exists; payer can pay |
| `occupy` *(new)* | physics | A person or entity takes up N slots of a kind (`dwelling`, `workplace`) in a completed structure, within capacity |
| `vacate` *(new)* | physics | An occupant leaves or is removed. Removing *someone else* is eviction; whether that's allowed is up to rules. |
| `create_entity` | mechanism | Kind is a vocabulary label, but not `person` or `boundary` (bodies and the world's edge are physics) |
| `dissolve_entity` | mechanism | Everything passes to the named successor (conservation needs the money to go *somewhere*; any other distribution is a rule). Persons and the boundary can't be dissolved. |
| `grant_claim` | mechanism | Records a claim: `{kind, holder, object, quantity?, until?, attrs}`. It means nothing by itself. |
| `transfer_claim` | mechanism | Claim exists |
| `revoke_claim` | mechanism | Claim exists and isn't held under an active agreement (the agreement governs it) |
| `propose_agreement` | mechanism | Parties exist and the proposer acts for one of them |
| `respond_agreement` | mechanism | Pending; responder acts for an unaccepted party |
| `terminate_agreement` | mechanism | Allowed by the agreement's *own* termination terms (data the parties agreed to) |
| `enact_rule` / `repeal_rule` | mechanism | The rule parses and passes the static checks (§6.5) |
| `message` | mechanism | Recipients exist |
| `decide` | mechanism | Proposal is open and awaiting this seat |

**Rights become claims.** A *claim* is a generic record ("X holds a `surface` claim over parcel p10"). The kernel attaches no meaning to it. Rules give claims meaning, for example "`construct` on a parcel is forbidden unless the payer holds a `surface` or `use` claim on it". Structure ownership is also a claim (`owns`), granted by a rule, not by the kernel.

**"Acting for" another entity is also law.** Stage 1 had a built-in chain of corporate control. In v0.2 a seat always acts as its principal (definitional). Whether it may touch another entity's money or claims is decided by rules. The example legal code's rule is "only an entity, or whoever holds a `control` claim over it, may spend its money".

## 4. Agreements: generic obligations, not built-in leases

An agreement is parties plus terms. Terms are data the parties agree to; the kernel executes them mechanically.

```json
{
  "template": "lease",
  "parties": ["devco", "bob"],
  "obligations": [
    { "payer": "bob", "payee": "devco", "amount": 180000, "every": 2592000,
      "first": "at_activation", "on_missed": "accrue" }
  ],
  "grants": [ { "kind": "use", "from": "devco", "to": "bob", "object": "p10", "quantity": 1 } ],
  "term": 31536000,
  "termination": [ { "party": "devco", "notice": 5184000 }, { "party": "bob", "notice": 2592000 } ],
  "attrs": {}
}
```

- **`template`** is just a label that rules can refer to ("rent caps apply to leases"). Templates (lease, employment) live in config as reusable shapes for the translator and for researchers; the kernel never interprets them.
- **Obligations** run like standing orders. Payment timing is a term, not law. If the payer can't pay, the kernel records a **`payment_missed`** event and a missed-payment count; with `on_missed: accrue` the debt carries forward. **Nothing else happens automatically.** Eviction, penalties and termination come from rules or from the other party's decision.
- **Grants** are claims created when the agreement activates and removed when it ends. That the lessor must actually hold what it grants is a rule, not physics.
- **Termination** is whatever the terms say. Rules can void agreements (the `end_agreement` effect, §6.3).

## 5. Mandates stay, alongside rules

Every act passes two gates: **the seat's mandate** (what this role may do: its office) and **the world's rules** (what anyone may do: the law). Both are config data interpreted by generic kernel machinery.

*Alternative:* merge mandates into rules (a rule with a seat condition and a "requires approval" effect). It's more uniform, but it would rewrite the Stage 1 mandate tests and the seat API's mandate field. **Recommendation: keep them separate for now** and revisit if they start duplicating each other.

## 6. Rules: the one generic mechanism for law

### 6.1 Shape

```json
{
  "id": "tenant-protection",
  "issuer": "city",
  "text": "A building cannot be demolished while anyone lives in it.",
  "when": { "before": ["demolish"] },
  "if": "occupants(block.structure) > 0",
  "then": { "forbid": {} }
}
```

- **`text`**: the law in plain language. Seats and researchers read this. The kernel ignores it.
- **`when`**: the trigger. One of:
  - `before: [blocks]`: a block is about to apply;
  - `on: [events]`: something just happened;
  - `moment: activation`: an agreement is about to activate;
  - `moment: payment`: an obligation is about to be paid.
- **`if`**: a condition in **CEL** (§6.4).
- **`then`**: the effect (§6.3).

### 6.2 Evaluation

- `before` and `moment: activation` rules are checked after the mandate and before physics. **Any** rule whose condition is true forbids the act, and the refusal names the rule (`forbidden_by_rule`, with `rule: <id>`). Rules don't override each other. To change a law, repeal it and enact a new one; this replaces Stage 1's "the latest zoning rule wins".
- `on` rules run after the triggering event, in enactment order. Their effects can trigger further rules, up to a **cascade depth of 8**. Beyond that, the effect is dropped and a `rule_cascade_limit` event is logged. The limit keeps every command finite.
- Rules also apply to acts produced by other rules' effects, so the whole legal system is consistent with itself.
- **Failure is closed and logged.** A `before` rule whose condition errors (e.g. integer overflow) forbids, with `rule_error`. An `on` rule whose effect fails logs `rule_failed`. Both are deterministic.

### 6.3 Effects

| Effect | Allowed on | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `forbid` | `before`, `activation` | Refuses the act |
| `act` | `on` | Executes blocks **as the rule's issuer** (the law acting), e.g. a tax transfer or passing a building with the land. Field values can be CEL expressions: `{"expr": "event.to"}`. These acts skip seat mandates (no seat is acting) but are still subject to physics and to other rules. |
| `end_agreement` | `on` | Voids an agreement by force of law, with a reason, e.g. eviction after repeated missed rent |
| `adjust` | `payment` | Changes the amount about to be paid, e.g. a rent cap clipping existing leases. The result must be between 0 and the original amount. |

### 6.4 The condition language: CEL

CEL (Common Expression Language, from Google; used for policy rules in Kubernetes and elsewhere) is a small, standard, deliberately non-Turing-complete language for conditions. Evaluation always terminates. It's implemented in Rust by the `cel` crate.

What conditions can see:
- `block` (the block being checked), `actor` (the acting seat's principal), `seat`, `now`;
- `event` for `on` rules, and `agreement` / `payment` for moments.

Kernel-provided functions (read-only; every list is returned in a fixed order):

| Function | Returns |
|---|---|
| `holds(holder, kind, object)` | Whether that claim exists |
| `claims(kind, object)` | The matching claims |
| `controls(a, b, kind)` | Whether `a` reaches `b` through a chain of `kind` claims (corporate control, for instance) |
| `entity(id)`, `structure(id)`, `structure_on(parcel)`, `catalogue(type)`, `agreement(id)` | Records |
| `occupants(structure)`, `balance(entity)` | Counts and amounts |
| `min_int(a, b)`, `max_int(a, b)` | Integer helpers |

Restrictions we enforce on top of CEL, checked when a rule is enacted:
- **integers only** (no decimal literals or conversions);
- no map iteration (for determinism);
- no time or regex extensions;
- a size limit on the expression.

*Alternative:* our own JSON expression tree. It gives full control and no dependency, but it's harder to read and write, and it's something new for researchers and LLMs to learn. **Recommendation: CEL (agreed, 2026-09-25), after a short spike** confirming the crate supports the integer-only restriction, custom functions, deterministic evaluation and compiling to WASM. If the spike fails, fall back to the JSON tree.

### 6.5 Who may make laws: the constitution is rules too

`enact_rule` and `repeal_rule` are blocks like any other, so rules can govern them:

```json
{ "id": "constitution-legislature", "issuer": "state",
  "text": "Only the city may make or repeal laws.",
  "when": { "before": ["enact_rule", "repeal_rule"] },
  "if": "actor != 'city'", "then": { "forbid": {} } }
```

Entrenchment ("this rule can't be repealed without X") is expressible the same way. A world with no constitutional rule lets anyone legislate. That's consistent with the anarchic default, and it's an interesting experimental condition in its own right.

### 6.6 Plain text and CEL must say the same thing

When a seat proposes a law in plain language, the translator produces both the `text` and the CEL. A mismatch is a real risk, and a way for a translator to manipulate outcomes, deliberately or not. Two mitigations:
1. Sign-off shows the seat the CEL **re-rendered into English by a deterministic renderer** (code, not an LLM), alongside the translator's text.
2. Both are logged, so measurement can audit mismatches after the fact.

## 7. Worked examples

These all live in config, in the example legal code or in Riverbend.

**Zoning**
```json
{ "id": "zone-river", "issuer": "city", "text": "On the river parcels only homes and parks may be built.",
  "when": { "before": ["construct"] },
  "if": "block.parcel in ['p10','p11','p12'] && !(catalogue(block.structure_type).category in ['residential','green'])",
  "then": { "forbid": {} } }
```

**Rent cap for new leases, and clipping existing ones**
```json
{ "id": "rent-cap-new", "issuer": "city", "text": "New leases may not charge more than $1,600 per home per 30 days.",
  "when": { "moment": "activation" },
  "if": "agreement.template == 'lease' && agreement.obligations.exists(o, o.amount * 2592000 > 160000 * o.every * agreement.grants[0].quantity)",
  "then": { "forbid": {} } }

{ "id": "rent-cap-all", "issuer": "city", "text": "Rent on p10 is capped at $1,000 per home per 30 days, including existing leases.",
  "when": { "moment": "payment" },
  "if": "agreement.template == 'lease' && agreement.grants[0].object == 'p10'",
  "then": { "adjust": "min_int(payment.amount, 100000 * payment.every * agreement.grants[0].quantity / 2592000)" } }
```

**Eviction after three missed payments**
```json
{ "id": "eviction", "issuer": "city", "text": "A lease ends after three missed rent payments.",
  "when": { "on": ["payment_missed"] },
  "if": "agreement(event.agreement).template == 'lease' && agreement(event.agreement).missed >= 3",
  "then": { "end_agreement": { "agreement": { "expr": "event.agreement" }, "reason": "breach" } } }
```
Even with that agreement ended, the tenant still *physically* occupies the flat. Removing them is a separate `vacate` act by the landlord, and a separate rule can forbid it without a court order.

**Ownership of money** (the example legal code's most basic law)
```json
{ "id": "property-money", "issuer": "state", "text": "Only an entity, or whoever controls it, may spend its money.",
  "when": { "before": ["transfer"] },
  "if": "!(block.from == actor || controls(actor, block.from, 'control'))",
  "then": { "forbid": {} } }
```

**Buildings pass with the land**
```json
{ "id": "fixtures", "issuer": "state", "text": "When land is sold, the seller's buildings on it go with it.",
  "when": { "on": ["claim_transferred"] },
  "if": "event.claim_kind == 'surface' && structure_on(event.object) != null && holds(event.from, 'owns', structure_on(event.object).id)",
  "then": { "act": [ { "block": "transfer_claim",
      "claim": { "expr": "claims('owns', structure_on(event.object).id)[0].id" },
      "to": { "expr": "event.to" } } ] } }
```

## 8. Coverage: every audit item, expressed as data

| Audit item | How it's expressed in v0.2 |
|---|---|
| B1 zoning, "unzoned = unrestricted" | `before construct` rule; no rule means no restriction, which is now the anarchic default rather than a built-in choice |
| B2 rent caps (new and all payments) | `activation` forbid rule; `payment` adjust rule |
| B3 breach on missed payment | `payment_missed` event plus an optional `on` rule; by default nothing happens |
| B4 no demolition with occupants | `before demolish` rule |
| B5 no sale while encumbered | `before transfer_claim` rule |
| B6 fixtures | `on claim_transferred` rule with an `act` effect |
| B7 derived claims not assignable | `before transfer_claim` rule on claims granted under agreements |
| B8 revocability | agreement terms, plus a `before revoke_claim` rule |
| B9 payment timing | obligation terms (`first`) |
| B10 only the holder can lease | `activation` rule: the grantor must hold what it grants |
| B11 building needs a claim | `before construct` rule |
| B12 only the holder can grant | `before grant_claim` rule |
| B13 dissolution law | kernel keeps only "everything to the named successor"; the rest via `before dissolve_entity` and `on entity_dissolved` rules |
| B14 corporate control | `control` claims plus the `property-money` rule and its siblings |
| B15 jurisdiction | constitutional rules on `enact_rule` |
| B16 which entity kinds seats may create | `before create_entity` rule (persons stay physics) |
| B17 termination rights | agreement terms |
| B18 no leases on unfinished buildings | `activation` rule; occupying an unfinished building stays physics |
| B19 one surface claim per parcel | `before grant_claim` rule, if a world wants it |

## 9. Costs and risks

- **Complexity moves to config.** A realistic world needs roughly 20–40 rules to be orderly. Mitigation: the example legal code, organised by area (property, tenancy, planning, corporate, constitutional), each rule with its plain text.
- **The translator's job gets harder.** Writing CEL from plain language is harder than writing a transfer. The re-rendering at sign-off (§6.6) and translation-failure logging are how we'll see where it breaks.
- **LLM seats have to read law.** The rules channel shows `text` and CEL. More law means longer prompts, a real cost, recorded per run.
- **The `cel` dependency.** Pin the exact version. A dependency upgrade that changes behaviour is caught by replay (D6), because logs are tied to the kernel build. Spike first (§6.4).
- **Performance.** Rules are indexed by trigger, and each is compiled once when enacted. Cost per act is the number of relevant rules times a short evaluation. Fine at v0 scale; to be measured with 1,000 citizens.
- **Scope creep.** This is VISION's "hardest block", which V0 deferred. D18 pulls it into the start of Stage 2, ahead of the LLM mayor loop. I think that's right, because everything else builds on it, but it is the largest single piece of Stage 2.

## 10. Invariants and tests

The four Stage 1 invariants carry over unchanged. New ones:
- **No act applies if a rule forbids it.** A property test with generated rules and an oracle that evaluates each rule's condition directly against the state before the act, separately from the kernel's own dispatch.
- **Rule evaluation terminates and is deterministic.** Property test over generated expressions, including the cascade limit.
- **The kernel contains no law.** A structural test: remove every rule from a world and assert that only physics refusals remain possible (the refusal codes left are physics or mechanism codes only).
- The Stage 1 law tests (`tests/laws.rs`) are rewritten against the **example legal code**, as tests of that config rather than of the kernel.

## 11. Decisions needed from Naz

1. **CEL** for conditions, subject to the spike: agreed 2026-09-25.
2. **Mandates stay separate from rules** (§5): recommended.
3. **Rule effects act as the rule's issuer and skip seat mandates**, but not physics or other rules (§6.3): recommended.
4. **Occupancy is physics**, via the new `occupy` and `vacate` blocks: recommended. People physically live somewhere, and eviction becomes a visible act.
5. **Refusal codes** for law are replaced by a generic `forbidden_by_rule` carrying the rule id. The law-specific codes (`zoning_violation`, `rent_cap_violation`, `structure_in_use`, …) are removed.
