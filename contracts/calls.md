# The call language (contracts v0.4)

A **call** is a structured intent: what a participant wants to bring about, named as a physical outcome, with the inputs the participant supplies. A translator turns plain language into calls, and code participants write them directly. The **resolver** turns a call into the participant's own acts (blocks). The full design and its reasoning are in [docs/design/translator-and-resolver.md](../docs/design/translator-and-resolver.md). This file is the reference, and [calls.schema.json](calls.schema.json) is the parsed form.

## Syntax

```
program  := line ( NEWLINE line )*
line     := [ NAME "=" ] call
          | "none(" LABEL ")"
call     := FUNC "(" [ arg ( "," arg )* ] ")"
arg      := KEY "=" value
value    := ID                    e.g.  lot-00-00   cell.3.4   apts-3/dwelling.2   acc-city   rec.412
          | NAME                  a variable bound on an earlier line of the same program
          | STRING                "…"  copied text: amounts, durations, free text
          | JSON                  {…} or […]: structured data (integers only), for statement data and claim contents; a list of ids (["a","b"]) where an argument takes several
          | call                  a nested call: an agreement's commitments (we_do, they_do)
```

- **One program is one atomic act.** Its lines are independent calls, or bindings the later lines refer to (things the participant creates itself in the same act).
- **One program is done in one capacity:** as the participant itself, or in one position it holds. The capacity is on the action (`as`), not in the calls: an act is one person doing one thing, in one role.
- **Ids are bare, and copied text is quoted.** Inside a quoted string, `\"` is a quote and `\\` a backslash. No id has a capital letter, so a bare word with one (`GreenCo`) is read as a name, as if quoted.
- **Keyword arguments only.**
- **Zero arithmetic.** Amounts and durations are always quoted text, converted by the parser's tables:
  - amounts, in the world's currency as its config writes it (`world.currencies`, the first: its `symbol` or code, its `unit_words` and `minor_words`, its `minor_units`), to an integer in its smallest unit. In a world whose currency is written `$`, with two decimal places and the words "dollars" and "cents": `"$500"`, `"$1,234.56"`, `"60k"`, `"$1.5k"`, `"$2m"`, `"500 dollars"`, `"250 cents"`. In one written `¥` with none: `"¥500"`, `"1.5k yen"`. With only a code: `"XYZ 500"`, `"500 XYZ"`. The sign, the code or a unit's word may stand before the digits or after them (`"500 €"`, `"KSh 500"`), and a sign several currencies share may carry the first two letters of the world's own code (`"MX$500"` where the code is MXN). Digits are grouped in threes, or with commas (or where the currency's `grouping` is `[3, 2]`) the last three and then twos (`"1,50,000"`); a comma and a point together are read by which comes last (`"1.234,56"` is `"1,234.56"`); a comma with one or two digits after it is a decimal comma. Where the currency says how its place writes numbers (`decimal_mark`, `group_mark`), a mark written once is what the currency says: in one whose decimal mark is a comma `"1.234"` is one thousand two hundred and thirty-four, `"1,234"` has three decimals (refused where the currency has two), and `"1.500k"` is one million five hundred thousand; in one whose decimal mark is a point, `"1.234"` is a decimal with a digit too many (refused). A space or an apostrophe between groups of digits is read only where the currency's `group_mark` is one. Bare digits are whole units; the multipliers are k, m, b, t, thousand to trillion, and the currency's own `number_words` (lakh and crore are read in a currency that lists them, and in no other); nothing finer than the smallest unit is read; another currency's sign, code or words aren't an amount (in a world whose `$` is the peso, `"US$500"` and `"500 dollars"` are refused); and what could be two numbers is not guessed: a point once with three digits after it (`"1.234"`), where the currency has fewer than three decimals and says neither its decimal mark nor that a point groups digits, is refused. Both parsers are held to one table (contracts/test/amounts.json). (The examples below are written in the first of these.)
  - durations: `"30 days"`, `"2 weeks"`, `"monthly"`, `"fortnightly"`, `"every 3 months"`, `"once a week"` → seconds. The world has no calendar: a month is 30 days and a year is 365.
  - when something done again and again starts or ends (`first`, `until`): a date as the world's papers write one (`"year 1, day 22"`), or a length of time (`"14 days"`, `"today"`), which is counted from when the paper is lodged.
- **Every call is checked against the argument table below** (the parsed form's schema, calls.schema.json). An unknown or missing argument means the call is refused as unclear; it is never silently ignored.
- **`none(LABEL)`** is the translator's refusal, with one of eight labels (a ninth, `ambiguous`, is the resolver's: below): `not_yours`, `needs_calculation`, `too_much`, `unclear`, `unknown_form`, `no_such_act` (what's asked is clear, and no function here brings it about: going somewhere to see for oneself, changing what an office does of its own accord; added 2026-10-06, when a fifth of the pilots' real requests turned out to be of this kind and were being forced into the nearest function), `not_an_act` (the request is to see, read or check something, not to bring anything about: looking is the participant's own to do, with what it's shown; added 2026-10-05, when a sixth of the pilots' failed requests turned out to be of this kind and were being answered "it isn't clear what should happen"), `look_first` (the request can't be written until something has been found out: it names what it acts on only by describing it ("any standing payment to …", "every notice about …") where an id is needed, or what it does depends on what a look would show; finding it is the participant's own to do, and the act is then asked for by id; added 2026-10-08, docs/design/call-language-gaps.md B.2 and B.3. Nothing is looked up for the participant, and the answer tells it nothing it didn't know).
- **`ambiguous`** is a ninth label, and the **resolver's** answer, never a translator's (added 2026-10-25, docs/design/names-and-dates.md section 4; the owner's ruling of 2026-10-23): the translator copies a name as written and does not know who exists. In a world whose people and bodies have names (the config's `names: true`), a name written where an id goes (an id slot given a name, a basis entry, a name in a list of ids such as a message's `to`) that matches more than one of those the seat has been shown is answered with a question and, beside it, `{ label: "ambiguous", name, for, candidates: [{ id, known_by }] }`: every candidate, and for each what tells them apart on the seat's own file (the post it is, the papers it was shown that name them, the accounts it knows they hold or asked to be paid into, the posts it knows are theirs; at most three things, nothing it was not shown). No guess is made. A name that matches nobody the seat knows is "No <name> is on file with this office." (or "... on your file." acting as itself), never looked up anywhere else. Matching is plain: the name as written, case aside, then without a leading "the" or a closing word for a post, then without its marks and with its spaces closed up only where that finds exactly one; names are matched in the order the place writes them, never reordered; nothing fuzzy. A program a translator writes as `none(ambiguous)` is refused as unclear (it has said something it cannot know). In a world without names the answers are the old ones, word for word. Both resolvers are held to one table (`contracts/test/names.json`).

## Functions

Each function names a physical outcome. The resolver decides nothing on the participant's behalf: missing decisions come back as questions; single physical answers are filled in; whoever else must physically act is asked (a `request`) or offered an agreement.

**Common argument meanings:**
- `for`: whose name the act is in (`speaks_for`); records are in the name of the entity it acts for unless `for` says otherwise. On `money`, `for` is the payment's purpose (its memo).
- `visible`: `public`, `own` (only the entity the act is made for: an entry on its own file, `statement(kind=entry, visible=own, text="…")`, which moves nothing and is shown to nobody else), or ids.
- **A hold** (`statement(hold=X)`, added 2026-10-08): an instruction to whoever acts on a standing record (a bank on a standing payment instruction) not to act on it while the hold stands. It resolves to `publish` of kind `hold` with data `{ "holds": [ids] }`, addressed to whoever X is addressed to as the participant knows it (or to `to`, if the call says), visible to them and to the entity it is made for; `text` may say why or until when, as words only (nobody watches a condition). One statement may hold several records. It is lifted by withdrawing it: `statement(withdraw=<the hold>)`. A hold stops nothing by itself and the kernel does nothing with it: whoever it is addressed to honours it, or doesn't, as their own conduct (the bank's: docs/behaviour/bank-payment-system.md). It ends no agreement and settles no debt: what the held payments were for is still owed.
- **A standing work order** (`work(task=…, at=…, every=…)`, added 2026-10-09; docs/behaviour/standing-work.md): work one's own staff are to do again and again. Payments have `every`; so has work. It resolves to `publish` of kind `work_order`, in the name of the entity it is made for, addressed to that entity and seen by it (and by anyone named in `by`), with data `{ "work": { "task", "at", "on"?: [ids], "by"?: [ids], "units"? }, "every": seconds, "first"?: seconds, "until"?: the moment it ends }`: `on` what it is done to (one id or a list), `by` who is to do it (none: whoever of the staff is there), `units` how much each time (none: as much as it takes, which the staff judge), `first` how long after it is lodged it starts (or `first_at`, the moment the day begins, where the call gave a date), `until` when it ends (written as a date, whose whole day it runs to, or as a length of time from now; kept as the moment it ends). Nothing is done when it is made and the kernel does nothing with it: the staff whose work it is follow it each time it falls due, in their own working hours, while it stands, and the work they do is `work` as ever, at its cost in their time. The latest order for a task is the one followed. It is changed by withdrawing it and making another (`statement(withdraw=<it>)`), and paused by a hold (`statement(hold=<it>)`): a hold on a paper addressed to one's own organisation goes on its own file, where its own staff read it. A survey or an investigation cannot be a standing order; `first` and `until` without `every` are asked about. **How often may be a published rhythm** (2026-10-09): an office's own procedure, a paper on its file, states each routine's rhythm (`data.routines`: for each task `every`, seconds, or `schedule`, the paper that sets when: a rating law's instalments). Where the request's words for how often are no length of time and name that paper ("as law-rates sets"), the order's data has `schedule: <the paper>` in place of `every`, and its staff keep that paper's time. Where the call gives neither how much nor how often and the procedure states the task's rhythm, it is a standing order at that rhythm (`every`, or `schedule`), marked `by_procedure: <the procedure>`. Words for how often that are neither are a question back that says what the procedure states. "Each working day" and "each school day" are a day.
- **A direction to an office** (added 2026-10-09; docs/behaviour/directions.md) is a form filled in: an office that takes written directions publishes a form for each (its `office` names it), and `form(form=works_staffing, to=works, recruit=no)` resolves to `publish` of kind `direction`, addressed to the office's organisation, citing the form. It changes nothing by itself and the kernel does nothing with it: the office follows a direction from those its organisation's own rules say may direct it, or doesn't, as its own conduct. It stands until replaced by another on the same form or withdrawn (`statement(withdraw=<the direction>)`).
- **Who in the office does it** (`done_by=`, added 2026-10-17, foundations F9): on any call (not `by`: a form may have a field of that name, and `work` and `structure` have one of their own), `done_by=<a person on the office's staff>`, or `done_by=<its staff's role>` ("the clerk") where exactly one is in post, has the act done by that person and on their time, and not the seat's. The platform's host takes it off before the call is resolved and gives it to the act (`by`, kernel: the actor, a system the organisation owns, a person in its posts, or a person employed by it). A name that is no one of the seat's staff, or a role with several in post, is a question back, in the town's words. The translator does not write it yet: its prompt has to learn it.
- **A paper of an office's own** (added 2026-10-09; docs/behaviour/office-papers.md) is a form filled in too: the office's stationery (a form with `writes`, known to whoever holds the office) says how its fields are written into the statement the rest of the world reads. `form(form=death_registration, deceased=c0242, notification=rec.17236, reported_by=c0243)` resolves to `publish` of kind `death_registration`, addressed, seen and citing as the form says. It may carry `text=`, the paper's own words in place of the form's. A field of type `list` takes several ids (`children=["c0003","c0004"]`), one of type `date` a day as reports write it (`from="year 1, day 40"`).
- **When a standing payment or a standing work order starts** (`first`; a date or a length of time since 2026-10-09): a date (`first="year 1, day 22"`, with or without its comma; `"day 22"` of the year it now is; `"tomorrow"`) is kept as that date, the moment the day begins (`first_at` in the request's or the order's data), and the request's text says it ("…, the first on year 1, day 22"). A length of time (`first="14 days"`, `"today"`: none) is counted from the moment the paper is lodged (`first`, seconds). The words round either are read past (`"from year 1, day 22"`, `"in 14 days"`, `"14 days from now"`). A date before today is a question back, with today's date; so is `first` on a payment asked for once (it is made when it is asked). With neither, a standing payment's first falls one whole period after it is lodged and a standing work order is in force at once. Whoever acts on the paper counts it so, and the draft's preview says the same (participants: `firstDue`). Both resolvers are held to `contracts/test/when.json`.
- **A count** (`quantity`, `units`, a form's `count` field): bare digits, a small number in words, or digits grouped as the world's currency says its place groups them, read exactly as an amount's digits are (`"60,000"`; `"60.000"` where a point groups digits; `"1,50,000"` where the place groups in twos; a multiplier, `"60k"`). A decimal, or a mark that could be read two ways, is not a count.
- `basis`: the records the act cites in support of itself (what it is done under, and the papers that back it), in the order cited: one record bare (`basis=rec.412`), or several as a list (`basis=["rec.412","rec.4019"]`; at most 64). A list of one is that record alone, and one named twice is cited once: one basis has one form (blocks and records: Basis). A record that is the thing acted on (the invoice a payment `settles`, the record a statement withdraws, the form filled in) is not also a basis.

## Examples

```
money(to=builder, amount="$500", for="the fence")
money(to=acc-landlord-1, amount="$850", every="monthly", for="rent")
structure(type=park, on=lot-00-00, by=greenco, price="$60,000", from=acc-parks, basis=rec.412)
form(to=planning, form=permit_application, lot=lot-00-00, structure_type=park)
agreement(with=c0042, template=lease, term="12 months", our_notice="60 days", their_notice="30 days",
          we_do=occupancy(who=c0042, in=apts-3/dwelling.2),
          they_do=money(to=acc-landlord-1, amount="$850", every="monthly", for="rent"))
t = entity(kind=trust, name="River Land Trust")
statement(kind=charter, for=t, text="The trust holds land for the town, in perpetuity.")
f = entity(kind=firm, name="Riverside Bakery", position="Owner")
position(title="Baker", role=staff)
position(appoint=bakery-baker, to=par.12)
presence(name="Ana Silva", age="31 years", template=newcomer, carrying="$2,400")
claim(position=liberty, holder=builder, about={"action":"build","object":"lot-12-03"})
statement(hold=rec.44)
statement(withdraw=rec.61)
work(task=collect, at=depot-1, every="7 days")
statement(kind=entry, visible=own, text="Tax withheld from the wages paid today, to be passed on at the month's end.", basis=rec.52)
none(not_yours)
```
